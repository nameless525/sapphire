### Draws

- **Per-view lists and runs** (D1, D2): every view walks a list compacted from the draw list's groups, and equal neighbours draw as one instanced call of `0..n`. Every tier runs every view that sorts its surfaces (`DrawRuns::EveryView`) but for the main view's and the insets' opaque surfaces and the prepass's opaque foliage; the phones run the opaque surfaces too (`DrawRuns::EveryViewOpaque`), behind a stated gate, and the desktops draw those a call a draw ("Everything deferred", "Draws and levels").
- **Culling** (D3, `sapphire_render::view_cull`): every view culls by bounds that are right, and frustum-only and no culling draw the same bytes.
- **Levels of detail** (D4–D6, spec §5.12): the coarsest cooked level whose error covers at most 2^bias output pixels, with hysteresis. `lod_bias` is live and `Rung::LodBias` is a rung of the ladder. Characters choose only where the stage lets them (`CharacterBody::detail`); a zone's cells take levels only when its sidecar asks.
- **Kept cascades** (D8): a cascade redraws only when its fit moved or its casters changed (`ShadowSettings::cache`, `Held` on every tier).
- **The mesh pool gives back** (D9): a trip's arrival frees the zone it left, unless it leads back there, and holds the wipe until the pool is at its floor.

## How to add

M3's first pillar is that a new enemy, encounter, stage, boss, cutscene or screen is documents ([m3-plan.md](m3-plan.md) §2). The recipes below say where that holds. **A step that still edits Rust is marked "Rust step", and each is listed under "Everything deferred" as a bug against the pillar.**

Every recipe ends the same way: `cargo xtask content` to cook, `cargo xtask replay` for the replays, the sandbox's replay lane for the app, and a commit. Then come x86-64's checksums from CI's artifact for any replay that's new or moved ([development-iteration.md](development-iteration.md), "Filling in x86-64's checksums"), which costs two CI rounds.

**A look step's `cargo run -p sapphire_sandbox`** reads the content beside the binary, `target/debug/content/`, which nothing writes, and fails with "failed to read pack shared". Point it at the cook: `SAPPHIRE_CONTENT_DIR=<checkout>/target/content/desktop/ cargo run -p sapphire_sandbox --features dev --locked -- …` (the exit run's rehearsal, finding 13). `cargo xtask run --platform desktop -- …` sets it up itself.

**What's left of Rust:** four steps: a screen showing a value no model holds, which is code by design; driving a character's dissolve, emission or flash from the game (M5 L11), which no document does yet; and two of a playable kind's (M5 C8), its graph under live tuning and a second armed kind's weapon after a lost device. ### A move

For the player, on a clip of its own ([m3-handoff.md](m3-handoff.md) has M2's version):

1. **Pose it.** Add a function in [tools/motion/moves.py](../tools/motion/moves.py) to its `MOVES`, and give it an ID in `frames.py`'s `IDS`. `--write` writes `<name>.keypose.ron` and its sidecar. `--write-numbers` puts `frames.py`'s draft frame numbers into the moves document. Read a sheet with `sheet.py`.
2. **Frame it.** Add a row to [player.moves.ron](../content/combat/player/player.moves.ron), last in `moves`, with `name`; `startup`, `active` and `recovery`, adding up to the clip's length; `motion`; `hitboxes` on path `blade`; `cancels`, `warps` and `cues`; and optionally `invulnerable`.

   A row has no intent. A press reaches a move through the top-level `intents` and `neutral`, or through another move's `cancels`. M3 added two optional fields: `cost`, against the fighter's `energy` (Q3c), and `cinematic`, naming a timeline (Q3b): `<name>.timeline.ron` beside the moves document, on the `Steps` clock. Every fight on the set loads it by that name (Z5a), and a name with no timeline there fails the scenario, naming the move.
3. **Its tests find it** (Z5e): [player_moves.rs](../tools/sapphire_pipeline/tests/player_moves.rs) reads the moves with a clip of their own from `player.moves.ron` (a `clip`, and `<name>.keypose.ron` beside the document) and the attacks from those with `hitboxes`. `each_move_plays_the_clip_of_its_name` holds every row: a move plays the key-pose document of its name, an assist the one its `source` names, and only a reaction has no clip. The clip's checks (feet, limits, root motion, the blade fastest on the hit) then hold the new move with no edit.
4. **Give it a row in the graph:** [character.animgraph.ron](../content/lookdev/character/character.animgraph.ron)'s `clips` and `combat.moves`. Without it, `every_move_a_fighter_has_is_in_its_graph` fails.
5. **Give any new cue a row** in [default_cues.ron](../crates/sapphire_cues/default_cues.ron). Without it, `every_cue_the_recordings_publish_has_a_row` fails. A warning the player reads in a fight is a mark of its own shape over the fighter (`at: Crown`), never a colour alone (M4 A5): `tests/marks.rs` holds each pair of marks apart under the three dichromacies.
6. **Cook and rewrite the simulation's copy,** `crates/sapphire_gameplay/baked/player.smoves`:
   ```bash
   cargo xtask content
   SAPPHIRE_BAKE_MOVES=1 cargo test -p sapphire_pipeline --test player_moves the_simulations_copy
   cargo xtask replay --update
   ```

It names `moves`, by ID; `brains`, by name, each with spacing, pace, patience, rest, `attacks` pressing intents by name, and `rise`; the starting `brain`; `phases`, in thousandths of health, where a phase may name a `brain` to change to; and `look`: `mesh`, `graph`, `held` (`None` when the weapon is skinned in), `scale`, `height`, `eyes_below_crown` and `framed_radius`. ### A playable kind (M5 C8, walked again by C12)

The worked examples are the heroine's [heroine.playable.ron](../content/combat/player/heroine.playable.ron), Aster's, in [content/combat/hero/](../content/combat/hero/), and Wren's, in [content/combat/wren/](../content/combat/wren/), the third, added by these steps with no Rust (M5 C12); [party-kinds.scenario.ron](../content/scenarios/party-kinds.scenario.ron) puts the first two in one party and [party-three-kinds.scenario.ron](../content/scenarios/party-three-kinds.scenario.ron) all three. Every step is documents.

1. **A figure.** Write `tools/figures/<name>.figure.ron` with a `character`, so its model is a VRM 1.0 file with its humanoid, expressions and springs, as [hero.figure.ron](../tools/figures/hero.figure.ron) is (B3's generator; "A kind of enemy", step 1, has the command). `the_committed_figures_are_generated` and `every_figure_cooks_under_its_budget` take it with no edit. The materials, ramps, ILMs, face SDF and sidecars are hand-written. The quick way to them, as Wren's were made: a scratch copy of `bench/` (its `district/` too, which `gen-bench` reads) whose `cast/cast.ron` lists only the new spec, copied into `cast/figures/`, with its `lods` and `merge` and `ci: Some((copies: 1))`; `sapphire_pipeline gen-bench --bench <scratch> --preset ci --out <scratch out>` writes them under `cast/<name>_00/`, and the model and maps there are the same bytes `gen-figures` writes. Copy the `.ron` documents and every `.meta` beside the generated files, every ID made fresh and consistently across the files. A model on another's skeleton (an enemy on the Lancer's, as the Ember Lancer is) adds `skeleton` and the `clips` humanoid map to its sidecar, as [lady.glb.meta](../content/ambient/lady/lady.glb.meta) does.
2. **Clips.** A kind may borrow another's: a key pose's sidecar names the new mesh's ID in `also`, and the clip cooks once more for it, as the heroine's locomotion, reactions, dodge and dash do for Aster and Wren, and her first two cuts and skill for Wren (her finisher keys her sleeves' morphs, so not it). The `tools/motion` writers keep a sidecar's `also` when they rewrite it (M5 C12; before that a `--write` would have unhooked every kind that borrowed). A key pose that moves a morph the mesh lacks fails its cook, naming it, so a clip on the heroine's own morphs (her jog, run and knockdown) can't be borrowed. Its own clips are key-posed (`tools/motion/`, Aster's `punch` and `kick`) or retargeted, as an enemy's are.
3. **Moves:** `<name>.moves.ron` with a `MoveSet` sidecar ([hero.moves.ron](../content/combat/hero/hero.moves.ron)), compiled into the simulation from its text as an enemy's are, with its hit volumes on paths of its own keyed by hand. The heroine's set can't be reused: its motion is baked from her clips (`player.smoves`), so a kind that borrows her clips keys its `root` by hand, copying each borrowed key pose's `root` key for key, its `root_turn` in degrees becoming `yaw` in radians, as Wren's is. To stand in a party it needs the intent `swap` and a move `swap_in`; a hit forces the warrior's five reactions by name.
4. **Graph:** `<name>.animgraph.ron`, a row for every move and reaction. A borrowed clip is the key pose's ID with `target:` the mesh's.
5. **The kind:** `<name>.playable.ron` with a `Playable` sidecar ([hero.playable.ron](../content/combat/hero/hero.playable.ron)). Its `name` is `character-name-<name>`, the key the HUD calls it by: add it to `content/loc/{en,ja}/game_ui.ftl`, and any new Han to `content/fonts/ui.charset.ron`. `moves` names its set; `look` its `mesh`, the mesh's `rig` (the cook refuses another and names the right one: `sapphire_pipeline cook --source <a folder holding only the document and its sidecar> --out <scratch> --cook desktop` says it in a moment), `graph` and `seed`, `held` (`None`, or a weapon's mesh, the joint that holds it, which the stage refuses if the rig lacks it, and its offset and rotation), `expression` (the face it rests on) and, from `version: 2`, `motion` (a motion of its own, "A cloth" below). Unknown fields fail; write `version: 3`, which has no `body_contacts` (M5 Z3: the joints a fight's body touches by are the fight's own, never a kind's).
6. **Into a scenario:** a cast member names it by its file's stem, `playable: "<name>"`, and plays the set its `moves` names, which has to be the kind's; `party.members` puts it in a slot. A member that names none is the heroine. In a party of kinds the HUD names each slot's member and calls the player by the one on the field.
7. **At the hero row, for the benches (M5 C12b):** a generated kind ships small; to measure it at spec §3.4's row, add its spec to [bench/kinds.ron](../bench/kinds.ron) with the detail and maps that reach the row and the exact triangles the generator then makes (`triangles` is a check, not a trim: a detail step that overshoots stays over, as Wren's 83,808 does), and `cargo xtask bench-content --preset budget --with-content` cooks it, under its shipped IDs, into the budget root (bench/README.md, "The kinds"). `every_budget_kind_cooks_at_its_row` (`sapphire_pipeline::bench::kinds`) names its cells.
8. **Known limits, no step:** live tuning's graph edits (`game_combat::tuning`'s `graph_of`) know the heroine's graph and the warrior's by path, so a new kind's graph (and every other kind of enemy's) takes a cook. After a lost device the app gives the fight back the heroine's sword (`set_blade_mesh` from the renderer's `sword`, `apps/sapphire_sandbox/src/app.rs`), so a kind holding a weapon of its own (`held`) would draw her blade until restarted; a skinned weapon, as Wren's staff is, isn't touched.

### A cloth: a skirt, a coat's tails, a fall of hair (M5 C4)

A playable kind wears neighbouring spring chains of its rig as one sheet of cloth. The worked example is Aster's skirt, [hero.motion.ron](../content/combat/hero/hero.motion.ron). No step edits Rust, and no replay moves: cloth is presentation.

1. **Chains to link.** A sheet is the rig's spring chains in order round or across it, each named once, so the VRM's springs need names of their own: a figure's chain with `around: Some((count: 12, step: 30.0))` makes `skirt_00` to `skirt_11` (B3's generator). The heroine's VRM names every hair strand `Hair` and every hem panel `TopsUpperLeg`, so no sheet can name hers. Chains of unequal length are fine: links pair joints by depth.
2. **The kind's own motion:** `<name>.motion.ron` with a `Motion` sidecar (`importer: Motion(())`), a `CharacterMotion` at `version: 2`: the shared document's `springs`, `feet_weight` and `look_weight` ([default_character_motion.ron](../crates/sapphire_anim/default_character_motion.ron)), then `cloth`, a list of sheets: `(chains: ["skirt_00", …], closed: true, lateral_compliance: 0.000001, bend_compliance: 0.0001)`. `closed` links the last chain back to the first, as round a body, and needs three chains; open needs two. A compliance is how far a link gives (0 is as stiff as the tier's iterations make it): lateral holds neighbours apart, bend two along keeps the sheet from folding across itself, so a bend a hundred times softer lets it flare. The cook refuses a sheet of too few chains, a chain named twice or empty, or a negative compliance, naming `cloth[i].chains[j]` or the field; unknown fields fail.
3. **Name it from the kind:** `motion: Some("<the motion's ID>")` in `<name>.playable.ron`'s `look`, which makes the kind document `version: 2` (a version 1 document reads as before, with no motion of its own). The cook refuses an ID that isn't a character motion; the stage refuses a chain the kind's rig lacks or has twice when it loads the kind, naming it. A kind that names none moves by the shared document, as before.
4. **Look at it:** `--look-dev hero --playable <name> --graph run` (or `turn`, `walk`, `cancel`), Metal captures as `lookdev-cloth-*` take them. Saving the motion while it plays takes it up in place, the tails where they are.
5. **What each tier solves:** Desktop-High 4 iterations a sub-step out to 20 m, Desktop-Low 2 to 12 m, Mobile-High 2 to 10 m and Mobile-Low 1 to 10 m; past that the links drop and the chains swing on as springs, coming back a tenth nearer. Those are `sapphire_anim::ClothQuality`'s, not the document's.

### A boss, with phases and a script

Walked. The worked example is [content/combat/knell_colossus/](../content/combat/knell_colossus/), and B3's test boss is [captain.enemy.ron](../content/combat/enemy/captain.enemy.ron).

1. **A kind of enemy, as above,** with three more things in its `.enemy.ron`:
   - `boss: true`, and its `name` is `boss-<name>` rather than `enemy-<name>`: the key the HUD draws on its bar (Z3e, M4 X2). The strings test holds it under `boss-`.
   - `phases` with names, `(at: 500, name: Some("tolling"))`. The hit that crosses a phase says its name as the boss's cue and changes the brain the phase names. The HUD's boss bar marks each phase. From there the music plays the score's phase of that name, if it has one (see "Music by encounter phase"). The fight marks the crossing over the boss's head by itself, the cue table's `named_phase` row (M4 A5), so a phase needs no row of its own; give its name one only for something more, and keep the name apart from any move's cue.
   - `script: "<id>"`, naming a `.luau` by its sidecar's ID. The sidecar's importer is `Script(())`.
2. **The script.** It returns `names` and its hooks: `on_phase(actor, phase, health)`, `on_move`, `on_hit`, `on_defeat` and `on_beat` are in [sapphire.d.luau](../crates/sapphire_script/sapphire.d.luau). **Phases count from 1, and so do places in `names`;** the walk's first try checked `phase == 0` and never fired. Commands such as `sapphire.set_brain(actor, place)` and `sapphire.force_move(actor, place)` take a place in `names`. A script is pure decisions, with no strings at run time (plan §3, "Luau's job"), and the scan refuses string concatenation and a bare `for` over a table.
3. **Into a zone, content only.** Add a spawner whose `spawns` is the kind, an encounter that calls it, and the scenario updates the next recipe names. The commit that does this must touch only `content/`, `tests/replays/` and `tests/golden/`, or `bosses_are_content.rs` fails. The boss's name must be in no `.rs` file, test messages included, and its script must be reached from the zone.
4. **Kinds with scripts can share a zone.** Each kind's script hears only its own fighters: their phases, moves and defeats, the hits they land or take, and every beat. A fight's scripts share one VM, with its memory limit and each step's budget. They also share one board of 64 numbers, as they share flags. So a script that keeps numbers on the board should use slots that no other script in its zones uses.
5. **Hot reload:** in a `dev` build, `link push <file>.luau` reloads the script into the running fight between steps, and the board is kept.

### An encounter in a zone

Walked, as part of both recipes above.

1. **The encounter:** `<name>.encounter.ron` beside the zone, with an `Encounter` sidecar ([market.encounter.ron](../content/zones/lantern_ward/market.encounter.ron)). It holds `starts_on`, a trigger's event; `faction`; `tokens`, how many may swing at once, and `token_rest`; and `waves`, each with `when` (`Start`, `Remaining(n)` or `After(ticks)`), a `gap` between spawns, and `spawners` by name.
2. **In the zone:** spawners for its fighters (`spawns` is the kind's stem); a trigger (`Box` or `Sphere`) whose `event` starts it; and its ID in `encounters`. A ground-level box must reach about 0.25 m under the paving, or feet never enter it. The cook refuses a spawner off the navmesh, and an encounter naming a spawner or trigger the zone lacks.
3. **Every scenario on that zone** (`content/scenarios/*.scenario.ron` whose `zone` is it) must list the new encounter in `encounters`, and in `cleared` if it shouldn't run there. A scenario that doesn't fails to set up.

   Its kinds needn't be listed anywhere else. The zone's spawners say what it spawns, so a scenario adds each kind that its encounters' spawners name after the kinds it lists, with that kind's moves and script. Cleared encounters count too. The kind is the `<spawns>.enemy.ron` under `content/combat/`, which the build compiles in. A kind's name is its file's, so two `.enemy.ron` files with one name fail the build. A name with no kind fails the scenario's setup, naming the spawner.

   A scenario's `max_actors` must hold its cast and every fighter its encounters bring, cleared ones aside, since a fallen fighter keeps its place: a game's flow refuses a scenario that's too small (`Game::new`, G1c), where the last arrivals would come to nobody and their encounter clear with no fight. `room_for_everyone_counts_the_cast_and_every_fighter_to_come` holds that count to what the zone plans for every scenario, and `ward_flow` holds the flow's run to its pool, so a fighter more in the ward means raising `ward-run`'s `max_actors`, a document's number.
4. **A scenario of its own:** a `cast` member with `moves: "player"` standing at a player spawner inside the trigger, as [ward-terrace.scenario.ron](../content/scenarios/ward-terrace.scenario.ron) does. Then record a replay:
   ```bash
   cargo xtask replay --record <name> --scenario <scenario> --script fight-back --ticks <n>
   ```
   The script is any document in `content/inputs/`, whose `fight-back` serves any encounter. An input none of them gives is a new `<name>.inputs.ron` there, which takes no Rust (M4 R2).
5. **Every replay on the zone moves,** because the zone's digest is in `tables_hash`. Re-stamp them with `cargo xtask replay --update`. The walk moved six. x86-64's lines are owed from CI. On the Lantern Ward, a fight that plays differently also needs `ward-whole-walk` recorded again by its recorder ([m4-handoff.md](m4-handoff.md), "Tools"), which is data. No test counts the ward's kinds or fighters (Z5e): `a_zones_encounters_bring_the_kinds_they_spawn` reads them from every zone's spawners.
6. **A golden** of the fight in its zone, if you want one: a row in `tests/golden/scenes.ron` with the replay's `--arena --replay` capture and a tick, and its PNG ("A golden"). Exit test 2 allows both in the boss's commit, since `tests/golden/` is one of the three directories it may touch.

### A zone, with checkpoints and exits

The worked examples are [content/zones/lantern_ward/](../content/zones/lantern_ward/), [proving_hub/](../content/zones/proving_hub/) and [proving_ground/](../content/zones/proving_ground/). Spec §5.21 is the reference.

1. **The zone:** `content/zones/<name>/<name>.zone.ron` with a `Zone` sidecar. The cook finds it by its suffix and packs `zone_<name>.spack`. The `ZoneDocument` denies unknown fields. It has `grid: (cell_size)`; `placements` of prefabs by GUID (`.assembly.ron`) and `instances` of meshes; `lights`; `probes` (a `.probes.ron` by ID); `day` and `rigs`, as relative paths; `colliders` (by ID); `navigation: Some((min, max, seeds))`, where `seeds` is optional; and `spawners`, `triggers`, `checkpoints`, `encounters`, `splines` and `exits`.
2. **Colliders:** `<name>.colliders.ron` with a `Colliders` sidecar. This is what bodies walk on and what the navmesh bakes from, since placements bring no colliders. A blocker needs its `groups` (`CharacterBlocker`, `CameraBlocker`); a `block_` or `camera_` prefix is only a convention, which a test holds for the arena alone.
3. **Checkpoints:** `(name, position, yaw_degrees, volume)`, reached outside a fight. A save names the latest. The cook refuses one off the navmesh.
4. **Exits:** `(name, to: "<zone id>", arrive: "<spawner there>", prefetch)`, with a trigger whose `event` is the exit's name. The cook reads the other zone to check `arrive`. A hub's `prefetch` brings the next zone in ahead of time.
5. **Levels for its cells (M5 D6), if it's large:** the zone's sidecar asks, `importer: Zone((lods: (levels: [(triangle_ratio: 0.5, max_error: 0.0005), …], mobile_skip: 0)))`, the mesh cook's `LodSettings`, its errors a share of a cell's mesh's extent. A zone that doesn't ask cooks one level a cell, as the shipped zones do; ask when cells are seen from far off (the benchmark district asks in `bench/district/district.ron`'s `cell_levels`). The cook locks every level's open border and every vertex on a line of the grid, so cells meet at any two levels; what that asks of pieces: a piece that meets the next cell's (ground, a wall carried on across the edge) puts its vertices on the grid's line, as `#quad` ground cut to the cell does, and ground two cells share has the same vertices along their edge. Nothing of the simulation moves: the `.szone`, the colliders and the navmesh are the same bytes.
6. **Look at it:** `--zone <name>[:<spawner>]` on the sandbox, with `--capture`. **At scale (M5 X1), the debug views answer:** what draws coarse too near or whole too far, `--debug-view levels`; which materials stream short of the level wanted (the pool too small, or a texture too big for what it covers), `textures`; what the main view and the shadows leave out, and by which test, `culling`, with `--debug-cull-from <x>,<y>,<z>,<yaw>,<pitch>` to see a pose's culling from outside it; which props don't share calls (a material or mesh apart from its neighbours'), `runs`; where fragments stack, `overdraw` (opaque by what the depth test keeps, the rest by invocation; effects not counted). The `counts` overlay panel (`--debug-overlay=counts`, a `dev` build) sets the frame's counts against §3.4's budgets. In the editor, `cargo xtask content --cook editor` first, then the zone pane moves placements.
7. **A scenario and a walk:** a scenario document on the zone, as above, with the zones a trip reaches in its `shelf`. The gameplay build finds every zone a scenario shelves, and the encounters beside it (M4 R3). A replay of a trip runs on the simulation's copy of each one's cook, since its navmesh takes a bake: take a new zone's copies once, into `crates/sapphire_gameplay/baked/zones/`, and again whenever the zone moves:
   ```bash
   cargo xtask content
   SAPPHIRE_BAKE_ZONES=1 cargo test -p sapphire_pipeline --test zone_baked
   ```
   Until then the build warns, and a scenario that shelves the zone fails its setup, saying how. `zone_baked` holds every copy to its documents. A trip in the game itself is data. A new zone is otherwise documents, held by the cook's checks. On the Lantern Ward, `zone_ward.rs` reads its checkpoints from the document (Z5e): each is a walk from the start, and no two share a name.

### A zone's look: its weather, sky, wind and fog

A zone's look is its own since M5 M6, as it opens and when a trip arrives in it: what it names replaces the stage's, and what it leaves out gives the stage's own back. It's all in the zone's presentation, so no replay moves. The worked example is [proving_hub.zone.ron](../content/zones/proving_hub/proving_hub.zone.ron), which the `zone-trip-hub` golden shows. Spec §5.21 ("As built (M5 M6)") is the reference.

1. **Its day and rigs:** `day: Some("<file>.ron")` and `rigs: Some("<file>.ron")`, paths relative to the zone, a complete `TodTimeline` and a `LightRigLibrary` ([proving_hub_day.ron](../content/zones/proving_hub/proving_hub_day.ron) holds one hour all day). The cook reads the day as the stage does and refuses one that isn't complete; the rigs it checks only by their document type, since their reader is `sapphire_world`'s, which it can't link, so `sapphire_stage`'s `zone_looks` test reads every shipped zone's rigs as the stage does. A zone whose look doesn't read fails to open, and a trip into it fails as it comes, before anything switches. To tune them live in a `dev` build, save the zone's own files and cook (`cargo xtask content`): the cook's key holds those files' bytes, so the zone recooks, hot reload remounts its pack, and the stage takes the new version's look. A save of the stage's own day or rigs while the zone's own is live waits for a zone that names none.
2. **Its probe grid:** `probes: Some("<id>")`, a `.probes.ron` with a `ProbeGrid` sidecar listing the zone's meshes as they're placed, its `timeline` the zone's day ([proving_hub.probes.ron](../content/zones/proving_hub/proving_hub.probes.ron)). A prefab whose mesh the grid lists must cook: no `cook: false` in its sidecar. Exits lead into and out of a zone with a grid.
3. **Its sky:** `sky: Some("<id>")`, a `.material.ron` of type `sapphire::render::SkyMaterial` with a `Material` sidecar, under the zone's folder so it packs with the zone. The cook refuses any other kind of material.
4. **Its weather, wind and fog:**
   ```ron
   wind: Some((direction: (-0.6, 0.0, 0.8), strength: 1.6, gustiness: 0.5)),
   weather: Some((wetness: 0.7, puddles: 0.3)),
   fog: Some((density: 1.5, height_base: Some(0.0))),
   ```
   Wind is the way it blows, which must have a horizontal part, and a strength up to 8. `gustiness` is a share and defaults to 0. Weather is two shares, `puddles` defaulting to 0, and holds all day unless the day keys its own: "A zone's weather and wet surfaces" below says how it draws. Fog's `density` scales the day's near, far and height layers (0 to 16, 1 by default). `height_base` puts the height layer's floor at that height in the zone's space. Leave it out to keep the day's.
5. **Its reflection probes** (M5 L2): a few, by hand, where a reflection would read wrong under the open sky alone: one in each interior or covered street, and one in each open place.
   ```ron
   reflections: [
       (name: "yard", position: (8.0, 1.6, 6.0), parallax: (min: (-8.0, 0.0, -8.0), max: (24.0, 12.0, 24.0))),
       (name: "shelter", position: (-24.0, 1.6, 30.0), parallax: (min: (-27.0, 0.0, 27.7), max: (-21.0, 3.5, 32.0))),
   ],
   ```
   A probe stands about eye height, and its box is the space it stands in: a room's walls, floor and ceiling, a street's two fronts, an open place's edges and a height over its roofs. The box must hold the probe, at least 0.1 m a side. Names are unique, and a zone holds at most 42. The cook bakes them under the zone's day (`day`, or the default day) from its cells, backdrop and instances, into `<zone>.sreflect` beside the `.szone`, and only the probes, the instances' places and what they see key it ([proving_ground.zone.ron](../content/zones/proving_ground/proving_ground.zone.ron)). Glossy surfaces reflect them since M5 L3: "A reflection probe" below says how to place one well and how to look at it.
6. **Its lamps' shadows** (M5 L16): a spot light casts a shadow when its shape says so, `shape: Spot(direction: (0.0, -1.0, 0.2), inner_degrees: 35.0, outer_degrees: 45.0, shadow: true)`. Flag the lamps a character walks under, at most 60° round the axis (a wider one casts nothing, with a warning). Desktop-High casts the 4 flagged spots nearest the view, Desktop-Low 2 and phones none, each a cached tile: a lamp costs a tile's draw only on the frames something in its cone moves (a character under it, foliage in the wind), and its lookup only on the pixels it lights. Count `spot tiles redrawn` on the zone's walk to see what yours cost.
7. **Take its copies again** (step 6 above), since the zone's cook moved. Then `cargo xtask replay` to show no checksum did. A golden of it is a row ("A golden").

### A reflection probe (M5 L3)

A probe is a row of the zone's `reflections` ("A zone's look", step 5): the cook bakes it and the zone reflects it, with no Rust step and no replay moved. Spec §5.12's "As built (M5 L3)" is the reference.

1. **Where it stands:** where a glossy floor, wet stone or metal would reflect something other than the open sky: a room, a covered street, a shelter, and one for each open place, so open ground reflects its own surroundings. About eye height, clear of walls; the cook refuses a probe inside geometry (more than a tenth of its rays meeting backfaces), naming it.
2. **Its box:** the space it stands in, as tight as the walls are. The environment reflects the probe of the box its pixel is in, corrected against that box, so a box larger than its room puts the walls in the wrong place: put its faces on the room's floor, walls and ceiling, which take the probe whole. Where boxes overlap or meet, a pixel takes the probe whose box holds it, the nearest of them where several do (by 1 m cells, two probes a cell); past every box it reflects the sky as before. A reflection fades to the sky past the box's faces over a quarter of the box's narrowest side, at most 0.5 m, so a wall's outer face within that reach takes some of the room's probe. A character reflects the probe whose box it stands in (the nearest probe where several reach it), whole, without the box's correction.
3. **Cook and look:** `cargo xtask content`, then `SAPPHIRE_CONTENT_DIR=<checkout>/target/content/desktop/ cargo run -p sapphire_sandbox --features dev --locked -- --zone <stem>:<x>,<y>,<z>,<yaw> --capture probe.png` stands the character at that place in the zone's space, the camera behind it; `--no-reflection-probes` takes the zone's probes away for the same shot, to compare, and `--time-of-day` picks the hour. The debug overlay's "Reflection probes" row counts the sets uploaded, their keys' bytes, the probes shown, the composed cubes' size and bytes, and the compositions so far. Views of the cell table and the boxes are X1's.
4. **What each tier draws:** Desktop-High and Desktop-Low reflect probes on the environment, corrected by the box, and on characters; Mobile-High on the environment without the correction, and on characters; Mobile-Low on characters only, from one cube a probe composed when the zone shows them, from the hour's heaviest key, at 32 texels.
5. **A golden** of it is a row ("A golden"), as the `reflect-*` scenes are. A scene from before probes that shows a zone with them keeps `--no-reflection-probes`.

### A zone's weather and wet surfaces (M5 L4)

Weather is data in three places and wet surfaces need no authoring to show it: no Rust step, no recook of materials from before it, and no replay moved, since the simulation never reads it. Spec §5.12's "Wet surfaces (M5 L4)" is the reference; the `wet-*` goldens are the worked examples.

1. **The zone's weather:** `weather: Some((wetness: 0.8, puddles: 0.4))` in its presentation ("A zone's look", step 4). `wetness` darkens and glosses; `puddles` is how far the ground's puddles have filled, 0 none and 1 every flat place under water. It holds all day and applies when the zone opens and when a trip arrives in it, under the trip's wipe; a zone with none is dry.
2. **A day that rains:** a `weather: Some((wetness: 1.0, puddles: 0.6))` in every key of the zone's day (an optional group: every key or none, as the post layers are). It lies over the zone's by the keys' share, so a shower at 16:00 is keys either side at the zone's weather; a time-of-day volume or a cutscene's day that keys it blends it in by its own weight. A day that keys none leaves the zone's.
3. **A material's response,** only where the default reads wrong. Left out, a rough dielectric soaks water up and darkens (paving, plaster, wood, cloth) and a smooth one or a metal takes the gloss alone; every flat, upward face may hold a puddle. `porosity: 0.0` to `1.0` overrides the first (glazed tile 0, unsealed stone 1), and `puddles: 0.0` keeps a material free of them (a table top, a raised walk), a part holding fewer. Old cooks keep the defaults: only an authored value changes a material's bytes.
4. **Look:** `cargo xtask content`, then the sandbox's `--weather <wetness>,<puddles>` sets the weather over the zone's and its day's for a shot (`--weather dry` takes it away, to compare). The puddles lie where the world puts them, the same for every material, so moving a prop moves it over other puddles, not its own.
5. **What each tier draws:** Desktop-High and Desktop-Low darken, gloss and stand puddles reflecting the probes (box-corrected) and the sky; Mobile-High the same, its probes uncorrected; Mobile-Low darkens only. A tier change keeps the weather.
6. **Its pipelines** are declared for you: a wet frame draws through environment pipelines of its own, swapped in for the whole frame, and the stage declares them (`PipelineInventory::wet_surfaces`, entry `wet`) when the zone's weather, its day's keys or `--weather` can be wet, and for a trip's next zone as it comes. A game that builds its own inventory sets `wet_surfaces` the same way; one that doesn't is told by the first-use counters on its first wet frame. `--inventory-without wet` proves the miss.
7. **A golden** of it is a row ("A golden"), as the `wet-*` scenes are. A scene from before wet surfaces that shows a wet zone keeps `--weather dry`, as `zone-trip-hub`, `zone-trip-ground` and `reflect-hub-square` do.

### Ambient characters in a zone (M5 C10)

People standing about a zone and walking its streets are its presentation alone: no actor, collider, nav agent or simulation random number, so no zone digest or replay checksum moves, and there's no Rust step. The worked example is the proving hub ([proving_hub.zone.ron](../content/zones/proving_hub/proving_hub.zone.ron)), which `zone-hub-people-*` show. Spec §5.21 ("As built (M5 C10)") is the reference.

1. **The cast and its graph.** A cast member is any skinned model whose clips the graph plays: the shipped cast (`content/ambient/`: a lady, a dock hand and a porter, generated figures on the Lancer's skeleton) plays `ambient.animgraph.ron`, which blends `stand` with the Lancer's walk and plays `shift` and `look_around` as gestures. A new body is a figure ("A kind of enemy", step 1) on the same joints, its sidecar's `skeleton` the Lancer's model, so every clip plays on it; its sidecars, material, ramp and ILM are hand-written as `content/ambient/<name>/`'s are. A new gesture is a key-pose clip in `tools/motion/ambient.py`, starting and ending in `stand`'s first pose, and a row in the graph's `combat.moves`.
2. **The section.** In the zone document:
   ```ron
   ambient: Some((
       seed: 11,
       graph: "<the graph's ID>",
       cast: [(name: "porter", mesh: "<its model's ID>")],
       posts: [
           (name: "east_post", position: (8.0, 0.0, 7.5), yaw_degrees: 227.0, who: Some("porter"),
            gestures: ["shift", "look_around"], every: (8.0, 16.0)),
       ],
       walks: [
           (name: "lap", points: [(-10.0, 0.0, -10.0), (10.0, 0.0, -10.0), (10.0, 0.0, 10.0)], closed: true,
            pace: 1.25, count: 3),
       ],
   )),
   ```
   A post is someone standing, facing `yaw_degrees` (from +Z, counter-clockwise seen from above), playing a gesture of the list every `every` seconds, least and most. A walk is a line walked round (`closed`) or there and back, each way on its right, by `count` people spread along it at `pace` m/s (0.2 to 3). No `who` picks from the cast by the seed. A cast member's `head` and `feet` default to `head`, `foot.l` and `foot.r`. Names are unique; a zone holds at most 16 cast members, 512 posts, 512 walks of 64 and 1,024 people. A person belongs to the cell their post stands in or their walk's middle lies in, so give a long street several walks.
3. **Cook and look:** `cargo xtask content` writes `<zone>.sambient` beside the `.szone`, which stays the bytes it was, and packs the cast with the zone. The cook names the zone, the post or walk and the field it refuses, and holds the graph and the cast to their kinds; `tests/zone_ambient.rs` holds that a shipped zone names what content has. `--zone <stem>:<spawner> --capture people.png` shows them; `--ambient off` takes them away for the same shot. The fight bench's count rows say how many stood and how many were in view, and its "ambient activation" row what choosing them cost.
4. **What each tier draws:** the tier's count of the people within its reach of the focus, nearest cells first, then by the seed (Desktop-High 60 within 72 m, Desktop-Low 60 within 60 m, Mobile-High 30 within 48 m, Mobile-Low 30 within 40 m). Who stands depends on the focus and nothing before, so a capture shows the same people at the same tick however it got there.
5. **A golden** of them is a row ("A golden"). A scene from before them in a zone that now has them pins `--ambient off`, as `zone-trip-*`, `trip-wipe-*`, `reflect-hub-square` and `wet-hub-dusk` do. Then `cargo xtask replay` to show no checksum moved.

6. **What a crowd costs, in counts (M5 C11):** a person a view draws is a skinned instance of 23 palette joints (the Lancer's skeleton, of which a figure uses 20) and 4,000 to 5,300 skinned vertices the frames C1's levels pose it, five draws a view (spec §5.21, "As built (M5 C10)"), and a capsule shadow. C1's levels pose 4 to 11 people a frame at the median at the tiers' caps, and nobody holds springs at any distance (the count row "ambient spring rigs" is never exercised). The bench's rows "ambient characters in view", "ambient in the middle band", "ambient in the far band" and "skinned instances above level 0" say where they stand and how many D5 draws coarser; spec §5.21's "As built (M5 C11)" has the district's table. Near people never draw coarser or pose less often, by C1's bands and D5's pixel rule, since they're the ones the eye reads.
7. **What would make the crowd cheaper:** D5 saves only what a figure's cooked levels drop (`<model>.glb.meta`'s `lods`: the shipped cast's level 1 is about 5% lighter, and the phones cook one level), so a deeper cook-side simplification of the figures is what pays, checked with D5's switch gates. A joint order for presentation alone (the joints a figure uses first, so a level skins a shorter palette) would save the two hand slots and the root today, and costs a remap between the simulation's skeleton and the drawn one in every place a joint is named. Poses shared by phase bucket were measured and not built (spec §5.13, "As built (M5 C11)"): walkers need hundreds of buckets a cycle to keep a pixel, so only people standing still would share.

### Which characters draw coarser, and a close-up (M5 D5)

A character draws at a coarser cooked level of detail only where the stage says it may: its body's `CharacterDetail::ByError`, which `sapphire_stage::scene::CharacterLevels` resolves each frame (spec §5.12, "As built (M5 D5)"). Everyone else draws level 0, the pictures of before.

No step edits Rust: a character's role says whether it may draw coarser, and a timeline's camera track says when everyone draws whole.

1. **Who chooses by error:** a zone's ambient characters (an entry in "Ambient characters in a zone"); every kind of enemy a fight stands (a `.enemy.ron` from "A kind of enemy", a boss's included) while nothing pins it; and a `--model` on the look-dev stage, a new enemy as a fight would draw it. A new kind of either needs no field for it.
2. **Who stays whole:** the player's character and the on-field party (a playable kind from "A playable kind" needs no field for it either), whatever they hold (a held item's zero-radius body never joins a character), everyone in an inset, and every character in a close-up (step 4). A fight holds its enemies whole while they're pinned (C1's list: the first fighter, a fixed one, one under a cue, one speaking), so an enemy a timeline, a move's cue or a conversation names stays whole for as long as it does.
3. **A character of several meshes** chooses one level for all of them, through the `CharacterBody` its parts share.
4. **A close-up:** a timeline with a `camera` track ("A cutscene"; a move's cinematic is one too) holds everyone whole for as long as its track holds the shot, and lets go as it blends back. Author the track; nothing else marks it. A shot without a timeline is engine work, not content: the code that frames it calls `Scene::set_close_up` as the fight does from its timeline (`game_combat`'s `present_timeline`), and a new place characters come from marks them with `CharacterLevels` as `spawn_warrior` does.
5. **Look at it:** `--lods <n>` holds every character that may draw coarser at level `n` (or its mesh's coarsest), `--lods off` draws everyone at level 0, `--character-lods off` keeps every character whole while static draws keep their levels (what the characters' levels save), and `--character-lods hero` lets the scene's own character draw coarser too, to see the hero's model as a crowd would draw it. A golden from before a new kind of coarser character pins `--character-lods off`, which leaves its static draws as they were.
6. **What it saves is the cook's:** a character saves only what its cooked levels drop. The shipped ones drop little (the ambient cast's level 1 is about 5% lighter, the crowds' enemy's 21%, and the phones' first level is the desktops' second), so D5 saves 3–4% of the district's skinned vertices on the desktops and nothing on the phones. Simplifying characters further in the cook is what would make it pay; the switch gates (`--lods <n>` against `<n+1>`) are how to check a coarser cook still holds the lines and the two-tone edge, and `render::level_switches` (`cargo test -p sapphire_render --test gpu render::level_switches:: -- --ignored`) holds them at every switch of the chains it reads from the enemies' and heroes' sidecars.

### A dialogue, with story flags

The worked example is [ward_guide.dialogue.ron](../content/dialogue/ward_guide.dialogue.ron). Spec §5.23 is the reference.

1. **Flags:** add the names to [content/flags.ron](../content/flags.ron), at most 256. A flag is an `i32` that reads 0 until set. The cook refuses a flag that isn't listed.
2. **The dialogue:** `content/dialogue/<name>.dialogue.ron` with a `Dialogue` sidecar. It holds `start`, optionally `opens_on` (an event name), and `nodes`. Each node has `say: (speaker, line, vo)` ("A voiced line" has `vo`), `set` flag writes, `next`, up to 4 `choices` (each with `line`, `to`, `when`, `set`) and `branch` arms with `otherwise`. A condition is a small language over flags: `flag.x >= 1 and not (flag.y == 2)`.
3. **Strings:** every key starts `dialogue-`. Add them to `content/loc/en/dialogue.ftl` and `content/loc/ja/dialogue.ftl`, and new Han to `content/fonts/ui.charset.ron`.
4. **Opening it:** the run's scenario lists it in `dialogues`, with `flags` naming the registry, as `ward-run` does. Then either a beat in [game.flow.ron](../content/game.flow.ron) opens it (`then: Dialogue("<id>")`, as the guide opens after the gate), or a zone trigger whose `event` is its `opens_on` does. The fight holds while it's open. `Game::new` refuses a flow whose beats name a dialogue its scenario doesn't load. In the shipped run, `ward_flow`'s `what_the_run_names_resolves_and_plays` cooks each dialogue `ward-run` names and walks it to its end, `plays_the_ward` counts what the scenario names, and the whole walk's recorder answers whatever the run opens (Z5e).
5. **Look at it:** `--ui-game dialogue --ui-dialogue <name>:<n>` previews the panel on `<name>.dialogue.ron`, `<n>` lines in; with no name it's the ward's guide, as the `game-guide-*` goldens show it. The sandbox's build finds each dialogue in `content/dialogue/` by its sidecar (Z5e). In the game: `cargo test -p game_app --features dev --test opens_the_guide -- --ignored`, and `plays_the_ward` for the flow's.

### A cutscene

The worked example is [court_gate.timeline.ron](../content/zones/lantern_ward/court_gate.timeline.ron). Spec §5.23 is the reference.

1. **The timeline:** `<name>.timeline.ron` with a `Timeline` sidecar. It holds `length`; `clock`, which is `Real` for a cutscene (it holds the simulation at a step's edge) or `Steps` for a cinematic in a fight; `camera`, with blends in and out and keys; `characters`, by role, playing rows of their graph and placed by keys; `cues`; `post` offsets; `subtitles` by key; `time_scale` (not on a `Real` clock); and gameplay `events`. Every cutscene can be skipped. There's no field for it, since only the time scale and events reach the simulation.
2. **A new pose:** a `.keypose.ron` and its sidecar, named as a clip and a move in [character.animgraph.ron](../content/lookdev/character/character.animgraph.ron).
3. **Subtitles:** keys and speakers in `content/loc/{en,ja}/cutscenes.ftl`, a line under `subtitle-` and a speaker under `speaker-`: the HUD shows only keys under those prefixes. `tools/sapphire_pipeline/tests/cutscene_strings.rs` and `game_ui --test strings` hold them.
4. **Look at it:** `--arena --timeline <id>` plays one on the sandbox, and the editor's sequencer pane scrubs it. It plays in the arena's space, so a zone's cutscene, whose shots are framed on the zone's places, frames no fighter there. Capture it by drawn frames, `--capture-frames <n>`: `--capture-ticks` alone draws none of the timeline (M5 Z2, finding 14).
5. **Starting it:** the run's scenario lists it in `timelines`, and a beat in [game.flow.ron](../content/game.flow.ron) plays it (`when: Started("<encounter>")`, `then: Timeline("<id>")`, with an optional `align` checkpoint the player is brought back to first), as the gate's does. `ward_flow` holds the shipped flow to what its scenario loads, and each timeline it names to its sidecar's ID and its place; the whole walk counts at least one cutscene (Z5e). A beat's timeline is recorded into the run's file, so the whole walk's replay holds it with no scripted input. A replay that plays it on a scenario with no flow is a scripted input document with a `PlayTimeline` at a tick (`content/inputs/`, M4 R2), which takes no Rust. Its goldens, if you want them, are rows in `tests/golden/scenes.ron` ("A golden").

### A face track (M5 C5)

Faces and looks are keys on a timeline's character track, and a conversation's line can set a face the same way. Spec §5.13's "As built (M5 C5)" is the reference. No step edits Rust, and no replay moves: faces and looks are presentation, and the timeline's simulation digest doesn't see them.

1. **The version:** a timeline document with faces or looks says `version: 2`. A version 1 document still loads, as one with none.
2. **Who:** a track's `role` is `hero` (the player), `foe` (who they face), or a kind of enemy by its document's `name` (`enemy-warrior`, `boss-captain`), which binds the first fighter of that kind on the stage.
3. **Faces:** `faces: [(at: 1.0, expression: "happy", weight: 0.8, blend: 0.3), (at: 3.0, expression: "surprised")]`. An expression is a VRM 1.0 preset by its VRM name (`happy`, `angry`, `sad`, `relaxed`, `surprised`, `aa`, `ih`, `ou`, `ee`, `oh`, `blink`, `blinkLeft`, `blinkRight`, `lookUp`, `lookDown`, `lookLeft`, `lookRight`, `neutral`). The cook refuses any other, naming the field. `weight` is 0 to 1 (1 if it doesn't say), and `blend` is in seconds from the face before (0.2 if it doesn't say). Each key holds until the next, and when the timeline ends the face blends back to its own resting face. The character's VRM decides what a preset does, its overrides too: `happy` on the shipped character turns the blink and the mouth down by its weight.
4. **Looks:** `looks: [(at: 0.5, look_at: Some((0.0, 1.5, 2.0))), (at: 1.5, look_at: Some((2.0, 1.5, 2.0)), ease: Smooth), (at: 4.5, look_at: None)]`. Points are in the timeline's own space, as its camera keys are. `None` hands the look back to who it was fighting. A fighter the simulation poses (the hero and their first foe in a fight with bodies) keeps its own look, as it keeps its own rows (spec §5.23's "As built (M5 C5)", "Can't do"); it still takes the faces.
5. **In a conversation:** a line's `say` takes `faces: [(role: "hero", expression: "happy", weight: 0.7)]`, up to four roles, in a `version: 2` dialogue document. The face holds until a later line sets that role's again, or the conversation ends. A timeline's face key for the same fighter comes first.
6. **Look at it:** `--look-dev face --face <preset>[:<weight>] --face-mouth <openness>` holds the character's close-up with a face, as the `face-*` goldens show it, and `--arena --timeline <id>` plays a timeline's faces on the fighters. That is a timeline whose shots frame the arena's own places: a zone's cutscene plays there in the arena's space, so its shots frame no fighter and its faces can't be seen (the court gate's, in the exit run's rehearsal). Capture it by drawn frames, `--capture-frames <n>`, as "A cutscene" says.

### A voiced line, with a moving mouth (M5 C6)

A conversation's line is voiced by a key of the voice lines' documents, and the cook gives each voice sound a mouth curve the speaker's face reads as it plays. Spec §5.15's "As built (M5 C6)" is the reference. No step edits Rust, and no replay moves.

1. **The sound:** a placeholder is a `tools/sounds/<key>.<language>.sound.ron` spec (`Line`, as `guide_greet`'s are) and `cargo run -p sapphire_soundgen`, then its row and SHA-256 in [SOURCE.md](../content/audio/generated/SOURCE.md); a recording is an `.ogg` with the plan's §3 provenance. Its sidecar says `importer: Sound((mouth: true))`: the voice lines' cook refuses a line whose sound has no curve, naming it.
2. **The cue and the key:** a `<key>.cue.ron` on the `Voice` bus beside it, and a row `(key: "<key>", cue: "<the cue's id>")` in each language's `.voice.ron`. A language with no row for the key plays nothing, counted.
3. **The line:** `say: (speaker: …, line: …, vo: "<key>", mouth: "<role>")`. `mouth` names whose mouth moves as a face track's role does (`hero`, `foe`, a kind's name) and needs a `vo`; leave it out for a speaker who isn't drawn, as the ward's guide is. The voice plays in the player's voice language from the frame the line shows, a pause holds it, and the next line or the conversation's end cuts it.
4. **Know its limits:** the curve's envelope is what a mouth moves by; its five visemes are a coarse spectral guess with no phonemes behind it (spec §5.15). A mouth needs an audio device: with none, or a run with no sound, it stays shut.

### An effect mesh (M5 L5)

An effect mesh is a layer of an effect in [default_effects.ron](../crates/sapphire_vfx/default_effects.ron), beside its emitters and trail. Spec §5.16's "As built (M5 L5)" is the reference, and `sapphire_vfx`'s `mesh` module docs have every field. No step edits Rust, and no replay moves: effects are presentation.

1. **The version:** a library with mesh layers says `version: 2`. A version 1 library still loads, as one with none, and writes as it did.
2. **The layer:** `meshes: [(name: "arc", shape: SlashArc, lifetime: 0.3, …)]`, up to four an effect. `shape` is one of the generated shapes, `SlashArc`, `Ring`, `Column`, `Dome` or `Card`, and there's no default. `delay` and `lifetime` are seconds on the effect's clock; `offset`, `rotation` (degrees, about Y, then X, then Z), `spin` (degrees a second about the layer's own Y) and `scale` place it in the effect's space. `color`, `alpha` and `scale` are curves over the layer's own life. `additive` and `soft` mean what they do for a sprite. `priority` decides who goes first past a tier's cap.
3. **The surface:** `pattern` is `Solid`, `Streaks` or `Noise`, repeated `tiling` times and scrolled by `scroll` (repeats a second, toward +u: along a slash from its tail to its head, round a ring the way a positive `spin` turns it). `skill_arc` is the worked example.
4. **An erosion:** `erosion: Some((mask: Noise, tiling: (3.0, 1.0), threshold: [(0.0, 0.0), (1.0, 1.0)], edge: 0.12, edge_color: (3.0, 2.0, 1.0)))` eats the layer where its mask is under the threshold, with a band of `edge_color` `edge` wide where it's eating. At 0 nothing is gone, at 1 everything is. `ember_dome` is the worked example.
5. **A flipbook:** `pattern: Burst` is a sheet of 4 by 4 frames, and needs `flipbook: Some((columns: 4, rows: 4, frames: 16))`, played once over the layer's life, or `timing: PerSecond(rate: 24.0, looped: true)`. Frames step, as drawn effects do; `blend: true` crossfades between them. A frame fills the shape, so it takes no `tiling` or `scroll`. `burst_card` is the worked example.
6. **Fire it:** a cue row names the effect as any other. `at: Facing` stands it at the subject's feet, turned to face where it faces, which a slash round a fighter wants: `skill_hit`'s row is the worked example. The fight declares the meshes' pipelines whenever its library has a layer, so nothing builds on first use; a library without one builds none of them.
7. **Look at it:** `--vfx mesh-scroll`, `mesh-erosion` and `mesh-flipbook` play the three shows the `vfxmesh-*` goldens capture, and a save under `--cues` reaches a running fight. Validation names the effect, the layer and the field, as an emitter's does.
8. **Rust steps:** a new shape, a new pattern or a cooked mesh by id (see "Everything deferred").

### A decal (M5 L7)

A ground decal is a layer of an effect in [default_effects.ron](../crates/sapphire_vfx/default_effects.ron), beside its emitters, trail and meshes. Spec §5.16's "As built (M5 L7)" is the reference, and `sapphire_vfx`'s `decal` module docs have every field. No step edits Rust, and no replay moves: decals are presentation.

1. **The version:** a library with decal layers says `version: 3`. Versions 1 and 2 still load, as libraries with none, and write as they did.
2. **The layer:** `decals: [(name: "reach", shape: Sector(radius: 2.6, angle: 110.0), lifetime: 0.75, …)]`, up to two an effect. `shape` is `Disc(radius)`, `Ring(inner, outer)`, `Sector(radius, angle, inner)` (degrees across, along the effect's +Z) or `Lane(width, length)` (from the origin along +Z), each at most 64 m, and there's no default. `offset` is metres across and ahead in the ground's plane; `delay` and `lifetime` are seconds on the effect's clock; `color` (linear, relative to the display), `alpha` and `fill` are curves over the layer's own life. `priority` decides who goes first past a tier's cap.
3. **The look:** `blend: Light` adds its color, bright enough to bloom; `blend: Darken` multiplies the ground toward it, as a scorch does. `outline` is a band of whole opacity inside the edge, metres; `body` is the share of opacity where the fill hasn't reached; `fill` grows from the origin out (a disc, ring or sector) or along a lane, 0 to 1. `noise` breaks the edge by up to that many metres; Mobile-Low draws a clean edge and shortens a decal's life to 0.6 of its own, a telegraph's excepted.
4. **A telegraph:** `until_strike: true` fills it for exactly what's left of its subject's wind-up, on the subject's own clock, so hitstop holds it and slow motion slows it; `linger` keeps it whole that long past the strike. `lifetime` is what it plays where no strike is coming. `telegraph_ground` is the worked example, its color the telegraph mark's (`telegraph.rs` in `sapphire_cues`'s tests holds them equal): one more reader of that mark's palette, never a second.
5. **A scorch:** `blend: Darken` with `noise` and an `offset` ahead of the subject. `scorch_mark` is the worked example.
6. **Fire it:** a cue row names the effect with `at: Ground`, which lays it at the subject's feet, its +Y the ground's normal where it stands and its +Z the subject's front. Only on the subject's own clock (`clock: Actor` on its own cue) does it learn the strike. `telegraph` and `heavy_land` are the worked rows. The fight declares the decals' pipelines whenever its library has a layer.
7. **Look at it:** `--vfx decal-slope` plays the show the `decal-slope` golden captures, `--cues` plays them in a fight, and a save under `--cues` reaches a running fight. `--decals off` keeps them from drawing. Validation names the effect, the layer and the field.
8. **Rust steps:** a new shape, a decal on a wall or over steps (a projected decal), a lit or textured decal (see "Everything deferred").

### A bend (M5 L6)

A bend is an effect mesh layer that moves the picture behind it instead of adding light: a shockwave's ring pushing the paving outward, a haze shimmering over a burn. Spec §5.16's "As built (M5 L6)" is the reference, and `sapphire_vfx`'s `mesh` module docs have every field. No step edits Rust, and no replay moves: bends are presentation.

1. **The version:** a library with a bend says `version: 4`. Versions 1 to 3 still load, as libraries with none, and write as they did.
2. **The layer:** a mesh layer (see "An effect mesh") with `distortion: Some((bend: Outward, strength: [(0.0, 0.0), (0.1, 0.03), (1.0, 0.006)]))`. `strength` is a curve over the layer's life, in shares of the frame's height, at most 0.1: 0.03 moves the picture 32 pixels at 1080 rows, the same at every size, render scale and tier. `bend: Outward` (the default) pushes the picture away from the layer's middle on screen by its coverage, so a ring bends only where its band passes; `bend: Shimmer` moves it each way by the layer's `pattern`, read twice apart, which averages to nothing, so scroll a `Noise` pattern for a haze. The shape, `scale`, `alpha`, `soft`, `pattern`, `tiling`, `scroll`, `erosion` and `flipbook` shape the bend as they shape a mesh's light.
3. **Bend only, or both:** leave out `color` and the layer bends without drawing anything; give both and it draws its light and bends too. A bend-only layer takes no random numbers and isn't counted as a mesh, so adding one to an effect moves no other effect's sparks; keep it inside its effect's life (a scorch's is 1.8 seconds on Mobile-Low) and the effect lives no longer for it. `scorch_mark`'s `shockwave` and `haze` are the worked examples.
4. **Fire it:** a cue row names the effect as any other; `heavy_land` is the worked row. The fight declares the bends' pipelines whenever its library has one. Bends draw at the camera motion setting's share and not at all when it's off.
5. **Where it shows:** Desktop-High and Desktop-Low into offsets at half size, Mobile-High at a quarter, up to 8, 8 and 4 at once by `priority`. Mobile-Low builds and draws none. A character, in front of the bend or behind it, and the interface never bend; every effect drawn that frame does, in front of the bend or behind, since the bend is one read of the finished frame (spec §5.16's "As built (M5 L6)" says why). So a character pixel that an effect covers whole bends with the effect, as the effect's.
6. **Look at it:** `--vfx distort-shockwave` and `distort-haze` play the shows the `distort-*` goldens capture, `--cues` plays them in a fight, and `--distortion off` keeps them from drawing (every older golden that could show one pins it). Validation names the effect, the layer and the field.
7. **Rust steps:** a sprite or ribbon that bends, refraction through a surface, a colour split along the bend, and bends on Mobile-Low (see "Everything deferred").

### A focus key (M5 L8)

Depth of field is a key on a timeline's camera, a cutscene's or a move's cinematic. Spec §5.12's "As built (M5 L8)" is the reference. No step edits Rust, and no replay moves: focus is presentation, and the timeline's simulation digest doesn't see it.

1. **The version:** a timeline document with focus says `version: 3`. Versions 1 and 2 still load, as ones with none.
2. **The key:** a camera key takes `focus: Some((on: Role("hero"), sharp: 0.3, near: 0.8, far: 4.0, blur: 0.012))`. `on` is `Distance(<metres>)`, `Point((x, y, z))` in the timeline's own space, or `Role("<role>")`, a fighter's head, named as a face track's role is. `sharp` is how far either side of it stays sharp, `near` and `far` the metres over which blur grows to its largest in front and behind, and `blur` that largest radius as a fraction of the picture's height, above 0 and at most 0.02. The cook refuses anything else, naming the field.
3. **Between keys:** focus eases with the camera's key (a `Cut` jumps), a key without focus blends from or to none, and when the timeline ends it fades with the camera's blend back. A role it can't find holds the last distance.
4. **Where it shows:** the desktop tiers. Mobile-High and Mobile-Low ignore it, at no cost. A move's effects (sprites, trails, effect meshes) draw over the blur and stay sharp wherever they are.
5. **Look at it:** `--look-dev hero --tier desktop-high --focus <distance>,<sharp>,<near>,<far>,<blur>` holds a focus on the look-dev close-up, as `dof-closeup` shows it, and `--arena --timeline <id>` plays a timeline's keys. Capture it by drawn frames: the rehearsal saw the court gate's focus key blur the court with `--capture-frames 330`, where `--capture-ticks 330` alone drew none of the timeline ("A cutscene", step 4).

### Light shafts in a day (M5 L13)

Light shafts are a group on a day's time-of-day keys. Spec §5.12's and §5.21's "As built (M5 L13)" are the reference. No step edits Rust, and no replay moves: shafts are presentation.

1. **The key:** a key takes `shafts: Some((strength: 0.35, tint: (1.0, 0.72, 0.45), length: 0.7))`. `strength` is the light at the sun in the units bloom adds (0.2 to 0.5 reads as a golden hour), `tint` a linear colour (white without it), `length` how far they reach from the sun in frame heights, above 0 and at most 1. Every key of the timeline sets the group or none does; set `strength: 0.0` at the hours without them, and they fade in and out with the blend.
2. **Bloom:** the same key needs its own `bloom` with an intensity above 0; the document's check refuses one without, naming `shafts.strength`.
3. **Where it shows:** a low sun in or near the frame, over open sky: they fade as the sun leaves the frame or sets. Desktop-High, Desktop-Low and Mobile-High draw them; Mobile-Low, and Mobile-High once its ladder sheds GTAO, draw none at no cost.
4. **Cost per tier:** two passes, a mask at an eighth and a march at a quarter of post's input (up to 160 taps a pixel, 81 at 1080 rows and length 0.6), and bloom's top level written at half floats on those frames. Longer shafts take more taps; a key with strength 0 draws and costs nothing.
5. **Look at it:** `--look-dev wide --time-of-day 18.5 --tier desktop-high --shafts-look <strength>,<length>[,<r>,<g>,<b>]` holds a look on the look-dev town, as `shafts-golden-hour` shows it; `--shafts off` takes a tier's away.

### A cut-in (M5 L10)

A cut-in is a live close-up of a character cut across the frame as a big move lands: an entry in [default_cutins.ron](../crates/sapphire_world/default_cutins.ron), named by a cue. Spec §5.17's "As built (M5 L10)" is the reference, and `sapphire_world`'s `cutin` module docs have every field. No step edits Rust, and no replay moves: a cut-in is presentation, and the simulation never knows one played.

1. **The entry:** `name`; optionally `kind`, a playable kind's asset ID, which wins over the entry of that name with none; `clip`, a clip of the kind's graph by its source's name, from `start` seconds at `rate`; `camera`, an `offset` and `look` off a `joint` (`Head`, `Chest`, `Hips`, `RightHand`, `LeftHand`) with a `roll` and `fov`; `light`, a light rig by name with a key and ambient, as a showcase's; `backdrop`, a color or a gradient `to` another along `angle`, with optional speed `lines`; `panel`, `Full` or a slanted `Band`, its `enter` and `exit` (seconds, a curve, and a side or `Centre`), its `hold`, and an optional `border`; and an optional `sound`. The `ultimate` entry is the worked example. Validation names the entry and the field.
2. **Ask for it:** a cue row with `cutin: Some("<name>")` in [default_cues.ron](../crates/sapphire_cues/default_cues.ron), fired from a move's `cues` or a timeline's `cues` track, as `ultimate_cutin` is from [ultimate.timeline.ron](../content/combat/player/ultimate.timeline.ron). A row naming a cut-in the library hasn't stops the fight's cues from loading, naming it. Only the player's cue or a timeline's plays one; an enemy's is counted as unplayable.
3. **Its clip:** the clip must be in the kind's graph's `clips` ([character.animgraph.ron](../content/lookdev/character/character.animgraph.ron)), as a move's is. One that isn't is counted as unplayable, and nothing draws.
4. **Look at it:** `--cues` plays a fight's cut-ins, and `--cutins off` plays none, which every golden from before L10 that fires one pins. The `cutin-*` goldens show a band, a full frame, the enter, a phone and flashes reduced.
5. **Frame it without a build:** `--cutin-library <ron>` plays a fight's cut-ins from a library document of your own, and `--cutins full` draws each across the whole frame.
6. **Rust steps:** a joint the five don't name; two characters, a weapon on the stand, or a painted or 2D cut-in are designs of their own (see "Everything deferred").

### A game screen

The worked examples are the six documents in [content/ui/](../content/ui/). Spec §5.17 is the reference.

1. **The screen:** `content/ui/<name>.ui.ron` with its sidecar, naming the model it reads, a list of `(path, kind)` that a cook holds every binding to: `menu.model.ron` (a way out, a run to continue, the player's settings), or `character`, `dialogue` or `hud.model.ron` for a screen showing those live values. Its nodes are laid out by `taffy`, with `key_from` for a key chosen at run time under a prefix, and it has `timelines`, `states` and `transitions`.
2. **Strings:** keys in `content/loc/{en,ja}/game_ui.ftl`, the Japanese under the file's machine-quality mark, and new Han in `content/fonts/ui.charset.ron`. Then run `cargo test -p game_ui --test strings --test menus`.
3. **Its row** in `content/ui/menus.ron` (M3 U9): its `name`, its `document` by the sidecar's ID, its `model` (`Menu` unless it reads another), `modal: false` only for words under the fight, what `back` does beyond its machine, and a row for each focusable node: `Command(..)` for the flow, `Open("<screen>")`, `Switch("<screen>")` for a tab that takes the screen's place, `Step(..)` for a setting, `Remap("<binding>")` to remap a binding (M4 A4), or `Nothing`. A remap row's glyph reads `remap.gamepad.<action>` or `remap.keyboard.<action>`, which the menus fill for each binding `sapphire_controls::remappable` lets a player remap in `combat_bindings.ron` (M4 X4): a new one is a named binding there, its row here, its node and its `remap-action-<action>` strings, and `menu.model.ron` gains its path (`the_documents_and_the_code_agree` names it). A row on another screen that opens it is how it's reached; that screen's document needs the node. `game_ui`'s menus and strings tests read the list, so they hold its nodes and keys with no change to them, and `a_new_screen_is_documents` is the worked example.

   Changing an existing screen's look, layout, words or motion is documents alone: the editor's screen pane shows an edit on the next frame, and live link reaches a running game. A `.model.ron` is in field order by path, so on a merge, rebuild it from its model (`UiModel::of_model` of `ScreenModel::sample`, which reads the glyphs from the bindings too).
4. **Look at it:** run
   ```bash
   cargo xtask run --platform desktop -- --capture <png> --ui-plain --ui-game <name> --ui-locale ja
   ```
   with `--ui-pseudo` for pseudo-localization. Goldens at 4:3, 16:9, 21:9 and the Deck's 16:10 are rows in `tests/golden/scenes.ron` on the `game-screen` base, each with its `--ui-game` screen and `--ui-locale` ("A golden").

### Music by encounter phase, and voice lines

The worked examples are `content/audio/generated/music/lantern.score.ron` and `content/audio/generated/voice/{en,ja}.voice.ron`. Spec §5.15 is the reference.

1. **A score:** a `Score` document with `tempo_bpm`, `beats_per_bar` and `bars`; `stems`, each with its `.ogg` by ID; `phases`, each naming the stems it plays; and `fade_beats`, `volume_db` and `duck_db`. Its sidecar's importer is `Score(())`. The phases a fight chooses are `waiting`, `running` and `cleared`, and the flow's `music` names those for the title, a defeat and a win. A boss's named phase plays in place of `running` once its health is past it, if the score has a phase of that name (Z5a): the Knell Colossus's `tolling` is the worked example. A phase change fades only the stems it changes, from the next bar line.
2. **Which score:** `music:` in [default_cues.ron](../crates/sapphire_cues/default_cues.ron), by ID. That's a data edit, compiled in.
3. **Voice lines:** each line is a `Cue` document on the `Voice` bus with its `.ogg`. Each language's `VoiceLines` document lists `(key, cue)`, and `voices:` in `default_cues.ron` names each language's document. A cue-table row speaks a line with `line: Some("<key>")` when the simulation publishes that row's cue. The voice language follows the player's language setting.
4. **A placeholder sound,** until a recording replaces it, is a spec, `tools/sounds/<name>.sound.ron`, which takes no Rust: a stem of the score's tempo, beats and bars playing one of the generator's instruments (a pad's chords, a pulse's notes, a drum pattern, a bell tune or a toll), or a line's syllables at a speaker's pitch (`tools/sapphire_soundgen`'s docs). `cargo run -p sapphire_soundgen` from the workspace root writes each missing `.ogg` to the `path` its spec names; add its row to the `SOURCE.md` beside it, which the generator's tests hold, and name it from the score or the line's cue. A recorded `.ogg` needs no spec.
5. **Rust steps:** a line spoken on anything but a published cue needs code, and a conversation's lines aren't voiced (see "Everything deferred").

### A dissolve, a flash or glass on a character

Since M5 L11 ([engine-spec.md](engine-spec.md) §5.12, "As built (M5 L11)").

1. **The look is the material's:** in a character's material document, a region's `opacity` (the share of its pixels drawn, 1 by default) and `glass` (a sky reflection over them, 0 by default), for a veil or a visor's lens; and the material's `dissolve: (scale, edge, edge_color)`, for how its dissolve breaks up and glows. Only a body's regions see through, and a blended material refuses them. `cargo xtask content` cooks it.
2. **Try it:** the sandbox's `--dissolve <0..1>`, `--emission-scale <scale>` and `--flash <r>,<g>,<b>` drive the character's materials, and `--glass` places two samples beside it.
3. **Rust step: driving it from the game.** `CharacterInstance::drive` takes a `sapphire_render::MaterialDrive` each frame, and only the stage's flags fill it: no document says "dissolve when defeated" or "flash on a hit" yet. A game that dissolves a character must also declare `fades` among its pipelines, as the stage does.

### A golden

A golden is a row and its PNG (M4 R1). The scenes are `tests/golden/scenes.ron`, whose header says what each field means and why each family of scenes pins what it pins.

1. **The row:** a block at the end of `scenes`, after a comment saying what it shows: its `name` (the PNG's stem, lowercase letters and hyphens), `ticks`, its `size` if not 640×360, a `base` if it shares one, and `args`, the rest of the sandbox's `--capture` command line. The name says its family, which `sapphire_testkit`'s scenes tests hold: only a `game-`, `tier-`, `display-text-` or HUD scene may be a size other than 640×360 (`only_the_hud_scenes_draw_the_interface_and_they_cover_spec_5_17_s_aspect_ratios`), so a game screen's is `game-<screen>-<aspect>-<locale>`, its aspect `tablet`, `wide`, `ultrawide` or `deck`; and a `-wide-` one needs its `-deck-` twin (`the_screens_scenes_cover_the_aspect_ratios_in_japanese`). Pin off what it isn't about, as its neighbours do, so a later feature doesn't move it; a `--game` scene pins `--tier`. Leave out `gates` unless a review chose its own, and `ci_seconds` until CI has timed it: it counts as the costliest scene meanwhile. Only `sapphire_testkit`'s tests check the list's rules (`cargo test -q -p sapphire_testkit --locked`, seconds), so run them before the capture: a scene whose name fits no family there pins `--ao off`, or the test gains a branch for its family saying why its picture needs the tier's own ambient occlusion (M5).
2. **Its Metal golden:** `cargo xtask golden --update --scene <name>`, which writes `tests/golden/<name>.metal.png`. Look at it. Its log in `target/golden/<name>.log` must have `first_use=0` on its "captured frame" line.
3. **The other backends,** from the PR's first CI run, which fails on their missing goldens and uploads the captures: each golden job's artifact holds `<name>.<family>.actual.png`, which becomes `tests/golden/<name>.<family>.png` for `vulkan-lavapipe` and `dx12-warp` once compared against the Metal golden by eye, and the scene's `ci_seconds` come from the same artifacts' `timings.json`, one per family. The testkit's tests then hold all three PNGs to the row.

### A patch

A patch is a release of the content that changes only what the simulation doesn't read: screens, strings, textures, meshes, sounds, effects, the HUD and the bindings (m4-plan §3, "What a patch may carry"). The flow, the flags, the targeting and body tuning and `game.documents.ron` ship only with an app build. M4 P4 is the reference; `cargo xtask patch rehearse` walks the whole road on this machine.

1. **The keys and the host,** once (the maintainer's, "Before a first upload"): `cargo xtask release-key new <file>` writes a new signing key to a file kept offline and prints its public half, and `cargo xtask release-key public <file>` prints it again (M4 X4); `SAPPHIRE_RELEASE_KEY=$(cat <file>)` signs with it. The public half of each signing seed goes in `platform/patch.toml`'s `release-keys`, and the URL the release is served from in its `host`, `https://` and ending in `/`. Every build after that trusts those keys and checks that host. A build made before has neither, and starts on what it shipped. `cargo xtask package` refuses a binary or a manifest holding the test key.
2. **The first release:** the build that ships carries a signed manifest, `cargo xtask package --platform <platform> --release-version 1 --expires <YYYY-MM-DD> --min-build <n>`, with the seeds in `SAPPHIRE_RELEASE_KEY`. Copy the package's `release/` to the host, `release.smanifest` last.
3. **The patch:** edit `content/`, then `cargo xtask release --cook <desktop|mobile> --out <host directory> --release-version <n+1> --expires <YYYY-MM-DD> --min-build <n> --patch-from <host directory>/manifests/<n>.smanifest`, again with the seeds in `SAPPHIRE_RELEASE_KEY`. It refuses, naming the file, a change the simulation reads, and a release the installed one wouldn't take. One release a cook: desktop and mobile each have their own host directory.
4. **Try it before it ships:** serve the directory with `cargo xtask patch serve <dir> --port <port>`, and start a `dev` build installed with the release before, with `--update-host http://127.0.0.1:<port>/` (the emulator after `adb reverse tcp:<port> tcp:<port>`). Its boot screen fetches, installs and stands on the patch, and the server prints the bytes each request moved. A release signed by a seed of the maintainer's needs a build that trusts it, so try a dev build signed with the test seed first (`patch rehearse` does).
5. **Ship it:** copy `packs/`, `files/` and `manifests/` to the host first, and `release.smanifest` last. A game that starts before the last copy finds the release before, and the next launch finds this one.

### A GPU

Since M4 T4. The table is [gpus.ron](../crates/sapphire_render/gpus.ron), whose header says how each family was placed.

1. **A row:** a `family` name, its `vendor`, the `names` that recognise it and a `ceiling`, the highest desktop tier it may take. A row only lowers a tier: the rules in `tiers.ron` still decide under it.
2. **Names match as runs of tokens,** case, `(R)`, `(TM)` and punctuation aside, and the longest match wins, so `GeForce RTX 2060 SUPER` can override `GeForce RTX 2060`. A table where two names could match one adapter without one holding the other is refused on load.
3. **Hold it:** `cargo test -p sapphire_render quality`. `golden_adapters_select_as_before_the_gpu_table` keeps lavapipe, WARP, the paravirtual Metal device and the Apple M5 matching no row, so no golden moves.

### A rung of the ladder, or a tier

Since M4 T2 and T3. The tiers are [tiers.ron](../crates/sapphire_render/tiers.ron), compiled in.

1. **A rung** is a row in a tier's `ladder`: `Scale` (a render scale), `Shed` (optional passes, each an `OptionalPass`), `FrameRate` (a cap) or `Tier` (a lower tier). The renderer refuses a ladder on load (`quality::ladder`'s `validate`) with more than 8 rungs, scales that don't fall within 0.25 to 1, a pass shed twice or one only a new renderer can shed (the cirrus or a wind level), a second cap or one not under the tier's rate, and a tier rung that isn't lower, of the same class and last. So every rung sheds in place.
2. **Goldens:** a `tier-*-rung-*` row in `tests/golden/scenes.ron` with `--rung <depth>` ("A golden"). Scene names take letters and hyphens only, so a depth is spelled out (`tier-face-desktop-high-rung-two`).
3. **Its walk is data:** `declares_every_setting` stands each shipped tier and holds each in-place rung, so a new rung or tier joins it with no edit. `look_tiers` holds the lines and the two-tone edge at each tier's floor.

### A setting

A setting is code by design, not a pillar recipe: it's a field of `PlayerSettings`.

1. **The field:** a new `VERSION` of `PlayerSettings` with an upgrade from the last, the old version frozen beside it, and its bytes held in `game/game_ui/tests/settings_formats.rs`.
2. **The row:** a `Step(..)` in `content/ui/menus.ron` on its page's document, and its strings in both languages. The menus walk and `declares_every_setting` find it by its row.
3. **What it changes:** presentation reaches the show through `GameShow::follow_presentation`. A setting that changes what a step reads enters the run's tables and is recorded as a replay command, as A1's auto-target strength and A6's timing assist are, and its default must equal what the committed replays ran on. `game_flow`'s `only_the_auto_target_strength_and_the_timing_assist_reach_a_run` holds every other setting to a fight's checksums and tables, so such a setting joins that test's two.

## Rules the reviews made

**M2's, M3's and M4's, still binding:** [m5-handoff.md](m5-handoff.md), "Rules the reviews made", has each with its test.

**M5's packages' own,** each held by a test or a scan:

- **The look comes first.** No level of detail, culled draw, run, streamed texture, tier or rung changes the lines, the two-tone edge, the ramps or the grade unless a row says so. An old golden pins a new feature off, so it stays byte-identical; each optimization draws the same image on and off, or within a stated gate where it can't ([m5-exit-run.md](m5-exit-run.md), exit test 3's table), and `render::level_switches` holds lines and the edge at every switch of the shipped chains.
- **A feature that is off builds and dispatches nothing,** and a new pipeline owner joins the inventory, the warm-up and `declares_every_setting`. Every pipeline goes through `PipelineBuilds` (`pipeline_builds::tests::every_pipeline_goes_through_the_route`, which lists the test files allowed to build their own by path).
- **Presentation moves no checksum:** levels, streaming, cloth, faces, voices, effects and the crowd are outside the step, and no committed replay moves with any of them on or off. A zone's, a timeline's or a kind's format changes only in its presentation half, with a test holding the digest; the crowd's own section cooks beside the `.szone`, never into it (C10).
- **The benchmark is generated:** `bench/` holds documents only, a generator runs on `sapphire_math::det` and repeats byte for byte, its cooks go under `target/`, the shipping modules never name it (B1's test), and a shipping build refuses a pack its release manifest doesn't name (B2).
- **A stage whose picture is compared or kept draws whole textures** (`Stage::whole_textures`): levels arrive over several frames by I/O timing, so two stages settled for the same frames can differ by a level, and temporal AA carries it further. Captures, still views and the scale lane's walks draw whole; only a run meant to stream leaves it off.
- **A test never trusts a cook it didn't make:** a pack in `target/content` or `target/bench` that is missing, or cooked at an older format version, is a skip that names `cargo xtask content`; a test that must have content cooks its own, small.
- **A new golden passes the scene list's rules,** which only `sapphire_testkit`'s tests check (`cargo test -q -p sapphire_testkit --locked`, seconds): among them, a scene whose name fits no family pins `--ao off` (`only_the_ambient_scenes_take_ambient_occlusion_and_a_grid`).

## Known limits and risks

- **Feel is unknown, and M5 built at scale on it.** Nobody has played review 3, M2's fight, M3's slice, M4's build or the district's fight. Everything M5 built serves any game on this engine, so a bad answer from a playtest costs M5 little but the party's HUD (C9).
- **No phone has run anything,** after six milestones. Every mobile number is this Mac's (an Apple M5) at the phone tiers' settings, the iOS Simulator's or the Android emulator's, and those two draw on the Mac's GPU.
- **The benchmark flatters.** Generated shapes and procedural textures lack an artist's overdraw, material variety and composition, and the cast's maps are scaled-up paints. A real district will cost more than this one, by an amount nobody can say until there is one.
- **The figures are one GPU's.** An Apple M5 is far faster than either phone tier's reference device, Metal overlaps its passes so no exclusive pass time is read here, and the run-to-run spread on this Mac was its load (B4). Every timed figure compares commits and tiers; none is a device's budget met.
- **The stock arena's bound bodies hash their graphs, clips, skeletons, rigs and the warrior's kind from packs,** which the release tool counts as patchable, so a patch touching that animation would change simulation state without an app build (M4's binding, measured in M5 Z3, not fixed). A recording made before such a patch is refused by its table hash. The fix is a cook-time list of what a simulation binds, or the bound bodies in a pack whose hash a patch may not change; either way their animation then ships only with an app build.
- **A cold boot on a busy machine can still hold one boot-screen frame over the limit** (X3; spec §3.7, "The cold boot's long frames, traced"): the frame waits on its own GPU work while the warm-up compiles and both core classes are fully busy. In one sitting 5 cold boots of 24 held such a frame; in the comparison that followed, none of 24 at the default pool did. No size or class of the warm-up pool shortens the wait. A timed `zero_hitches` traces every boot-screen frame over half the interval, so a boot can still show where a long frame's time went: its `advance`, layout and `submit`, its wait for its own GPU work, the compiles in flight and the cores' busy share. On a device, read a cold boot's worst boot-screen frame under the lane's settings (`SAPPHIRE_HITCH_WARM_UP_THREADS`, `SAPPHIRE_HITCH_WARM_UP_CLASS`, `SAPPHIRE_HITCH_FRAME_CLASS`) before changing the pool there ([m5-handoff.md](m5-handoff.md), "Exit test 1's margin").
- **Packs are as large as their content** (M7): a district of real content in one pack makes every patch to it as slow as the 1.1 GB one, until packs are capped.
- **Pixels move by accident on this GPU:** an instanced call's derivatives, a uniform branch around new shader code in the untaken path (L4: six `game-ward-gate-*` scenes, up to 13 code values; an override constant at pipeline build is the exact switch), draw order at depth ties, and captures that differ run to run. Each was contained by a rule or a gate; the next GPU may show others.
- **Cooks differ across architectures** in textures and their levels, so where a release is cooked decides what a patch moves ([m5-handoff.md](m5-handoff.md), "Patches").
- **A moved checksum costs two CI rounds,** because no machine here runs x86-64.
- **The stress fight's flashes and the ultimate with its cut-in both sit on WCAG 2.3.1's limit by default,** 6 transitions in their worst second: a flash added to either fails M4's exit test 6.
- **M4's risks carry:** [m5-handoff.md](m5-handoff.md)'s "Known limits and risks" has the stores' yearly moves, "collects nothing" resting on the patch host, export compliance, a cold emulator's boot screen, text short of WCAG 1.4.3's contrast, and motion without an animator, machine translation, our own navigation runtime, a VM in the step, software rasterizers in place of GPUs and scenarios compiled in.
