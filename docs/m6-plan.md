# M6 plan: performance at the budgets

- **Status:** proposed on 2026-11-17. The maintainer asked for the performance findings to become M6's work packages, as the earlier milestones' plans did with theirs, and is still deciding what else M6 holds. M6 starts when the maintainer approves this plan. Every decision in §3 was taken by the agent who wrote it, and each can be undone by saying so (§7).
- **Milestone:** M6, which lands as release 0.3.0. It builds on 0.2.0 (tag `v0.2.0`): M0 to M5 built, M5 closed with its exit test 1 not met on Desktop-High by 0.03 ms of GPU frame on one valid round ([m6-handoff.md](m6-handoff.md)), nothing played by a person, and nothing run on a phone or on a Windows or Linux GPU.
- **Inputs:**
  - The spec: §3's tiers and budgets (all still proposals) and its measured sections (§3.5 to §3.8), the renderer's module spec (§5.12), §5.16 and §5.17's measured costs, and §9's gates.
  - [m6-handoff.md](m6-handoff.md): "What M6 inherits from the exit run", "Rules the reviews made" and "Everything deferred".
  - [m5-exit-run.md](m5-exit-run.md), "Final run at f4efa213e": the verdicts, their figures, the rule for a void round and what the run found.
  - The maintainer's performance research of 2026-11-16 and 17, kept outside the repository: a read of the code and of M5's final batch, ranking where the frame goes and what to change. This plan carries its findings in its own words. Its engine facts are cited here by file and line at `main` = `551977f30`, whose code is v0.2.0's (the commits after the tag change documents only), and its figures are cited to the tracked record or marked as §1 says.

## 1. What M6 is for

M6 answers one question: **can the engine hold its frame rows with headroom in the fight that ships, and can its measurements say where every millisecond goes?**

0.2.0 can't say either:

- its one fail is Desktop-High's district fight, 12.03 ms of GPU frame at p95 against 12.00, resting on one valid round ([m5-exit-run.md](m5-exit-run.md), "Final run at f4efa213e");
- that fight is a lower bound on the one a game ships: it plays no combat effect, no cut-in, no probe grid, no reflection probe and no daylight;
- the report reading the batch takes each statistic's least over every round, void rounds too, and Metal's pass rows are tails that overlap, so no figure today says what one pass costs;
- every phone figure is this Mac at a phone tier's settings.

M6 first makes the measurement able to judge the work, then measures the fight that ships, then cuts the frame where the measurement says, each change priced by a probe before it is built and stopped by a stated criterion if the price is too small. It adds no feature and no game (§3).

**Marks used in this plan:**

- **[M]** measured on the M5 Mac (an Apple M5, 24 GB) at the named tier's settings, size and cook. A measured figure cites its tracked record where it has one. One marked **[M, logs]** is read from M5's final batch's logs at f4efa213e, which are kept beside the batch outside the repository; B1's report prints such figures.
- **[E]** estimated from measured rows.
- **[U]** unverified: read from the code, a vendor's document or wgpu's changelog, and not measured here.

**Every phone figure is the Mac at phone settings,** never a phone's. On Metal a pass row is an exclusive tail, a pass's end minus the latest end before it (`crates/sapphire_render/src/graph/timing.rs:38-51`): rows sum to the frame, but a pass that runs beside another is charged only for what sticks out, so only the GPU frame is exact.

What that says:

- **The main pass is 42 to 66% of the GPU frame,** and in the fights about half of it is anime character shading. Between the district fight's ticks 301 and 801 on Desktop-High the main pass rises 2.21 ms for 623,000 more character pixels [M, logs]. Part of that rise doesn't scale with pixels, so a character pixel costs roughly 3 to 5 times the environment pixel it covers, and the characters are about 2.4 to 2.6 ms of the busiest frame's 6.16 ms main pass [E]. Counted from the shader, a body pixel by day does about 17 texture reads and 390 arithmetic operations with no local light: the largest groups are the shadow taps (9 cascade taps, 18 in a blend band, and 9 more for each received character's map) and the material maps' manual two-level reads.
- **Depth-only work is next:** the character prepass, the scene prepass and the cascades, 15 to 23% of the desktops' frames [M, logs], and 4.01 ms at p95 on Desktop-High's exit walk while the hour runs, when every light step redraws the cascades.
- **On the desktops the resolution-bound passes follow** (temporal AA, clustering with GTAO, post): 26 to 33% of Desktop-High's frame [M, logs].
- **On the phone tiers, the output-size upscaler tail** is 15 to 23% of the frame [M, logs]. Its two passes run at the output's size whatever the scene's: 0.73 to 0.74 ms in look-dev, 0.40 to 0.48 ms more than bilinear (spec §5.12, "As built (M5 L14)").
- **The CPU matters only through tails,** each a single frame, which a p95 never shows: a fight's restart at 7.85 to 7.88 ms and about 11,100 allocations [M, logs] (a median of 8.4 to 9.2 ms in the game, spec §3.5); a spring rig's entry at tick 1001, where joints go from 442 to 631 and secondary motion takes 1.57 ms on Desktop-Low against its 0.5 ms row and 1.53 on Mobile-Low against 1.0 [M, logs]; the texture streamer's worst, 0.815 ms on Desktop-Low against 0.5 [M, logs]; and frames whose thread sat on an efficiency core, 8 to 21% of the top 1% of `record` frames against at most 0.1% of the rest, among them a 28.2 ms Desktop-Low walk frame the report's least-of-rounds hid [M, logs].
- **What M5's features bought,** valid rounds only [M, logs]: capsule bins 0.8 to 1.5 ms a tier, the largest measured shading win; the held shadow cache 0.58 / 0.98 ms p50 / p95 on Desktop-Low; levels of detail 0.42 / 0.89 ms on Desktop-Low and at most 0.12 on the phones, for 0.08 to 0.23 ms of `render` CPU; character levels nothing measurable; draw runs 0.10 to 0.27 ms of `render` CPU and no GPU change. Geometry, submission and visibility convert poorly into time on this GPU; per-pixel character work converts well.

**What the benchmark can't see,** each able to hide a millisecond or more:

- **No combat effect:** `gpu_vfx_us` reads 0 on every traced frame [M, logs]; the spec's particles-and-distortion row is 1.2 ms on Desktop-Low and 3.0 on Mobile-Low (§3.3).
- **No cut-in:** measured outside the batch at f4efa213e, a cut-in adds 1.02 ms of GPU and 1.05 ms of CPU on Desktop-High at 1920×1080 while it plays (spec §5.17, L10's costs) [M].
- **No daylight:** the district's day has the sun at intensity 0 in both keys (`bench/district/district_day.ron`).
- **No probe grid and no reflection probe:** the generator writes `probes: None` and no reflections (`tools/sapphire_pipeline/src/bench/district.rs:450-462`). The grid's per-pixel lookup measured 0.6 to 1.0 ms of Desktop-High's 1080p frame in look-dev (spec §5.12, the ambient grid's "Where it goes") [M], about 1.1 to 1.8 ms at 1440p [E].
- **The phone tiers ran the desktop's CPU:** nine compute workers on this Mac, where a phone has its performance cores less one (`crates/sapphire_jobs/src/topology.rs:54-58`).
- **No present:** the bench waits for the GPU every frame (`apps/sapphire_sandbox/src/bench.rs:413`), so pacing, latency, input and heat aren't read.
- **No phone:** a phone's GPU is several times slower by a factor nobody has measured, so the phone ranking in §5 uses shares of the frame, not milliseconds.

Smaller distortions: twelve enemies on the phone tiers where their row says six (spec §3.4); zone walks keep Desktop-High's spring quality on every tier ([m6-handoff.md](m6-handoff.md), "The benchmark and its measurement"); the shipped game never turns the texture streamer on (the handoff's "Memory, streaming and patches"); the `kept` cascade mode was never timed; and the generated content flatters every row.

So **the measured fight is a lower bound.** With a probe grid's lookup on every frame and a cut-in on the frames it plays, Desktop-High's shipped fight lands near 13 to 15 ms at p95 [E], 1 to 3 ms over its line rather than 0.03, with effects, unmeasured, on top. B4 and B5 replace that estimate with a measurement.

### What a one-genre engine may assume, and what each buys

A general engine must shade any material under any light from any direction, filter every texture's continuous detail, and keep the world drawn behind any screen. An engine for anime-aesthetic action games may assume the opposite where the code shows the assumption carries weight. Only those a package uses are listed.

- **Characters are few, and they are the hero content:** 15 to 19% of a fight's pixels [M, logs], each dearer than an environment pixel. So the anime character shader and its hull can be cut term by term, the hero and close-ups pinned whole and the rest coarsened by pixel density (P1 to P5, F4, K3, K4).
- **Light is authored:** one key light on a schedule, ramps and bands, a light rig; another character's shadow is a soft darkening under the two-tone split. So the shadow light can latch (S1, S2), received shadows can be bounded, the hull can take one shadow tap, face light can be hoisted per character, and lesser lights can fold into a rig fill (P1 to P3, P6).
- **The frame is mostly flat colour regions with hard edges.** So flat maps can read one whole level (P4), the upscaler can take a one-tap path in flat groups (F1, F2), and a control map constant per region can fold into the material (K4).
- **Fights pause under menus, zone trips have wipes, and a restart can sit behind a fade.** So the scene can be held instead of drawn (F7), probes composed under the wipe (E4), and the restart spread behind the fade (J2).
- **Effects follow temporal AA's resolve and never enter its history.** So they can draw into a second output of the resolve with no whole-frame copy (F6, E1).
- **One lead is in close view,** and secondary motion's clearance pass is fitted to the lead's garment. So figures off the lead and the phone tiers can solve on coarser intervals (J1).

**Lines are the look,** so the same specialization rules things out. Variable-rate and half-rate shading staircase the two-tone split, which takes `fwidth` (`crates/sapphire_render/shaders/anime_character.wgsl:1333`), and moving lines keep no temporal history (`crates/sapphire_render/src/temporal.rs:40-51`), so nothing may lean on history for lines. The specialization buys no saving on a shaded pixel that a tuned general engine drawing this look wouldn't also make; its lead is in which pixels it may cheapen exactly, and in tails, hitches and power.

### Exit tests

Each is a proposal, judged on the M5 Mac as M5's were, alone on the machine with the display asleep, by the rule for a void round in [m5-exit-run.md](m5-exit-run.md) (or B1's reading, if §3's decision on it is taken). Each says what this run can prove and what it can't. **Owed in every test:** the phones, a Vulkan or DX12 GPU's exclusive pass times, and an M1 to M4 Mac.

1. **The measurement judges the work (new).** The report reads valid rounds only: each per-run table shows every round with its lock, void rounds marked, and a least-of-rounds figure only as a labelled diagnostic. A serial run times each pass alone on Metal. Frames the bench leaves out (a restart) are rows of their own. Every M5 unit that had no valid round has one (B0).
   - **Proved here:** the report's tables, re-read over M5's final logs with no verdict moving, and a serial run printing every pass.
   - **Owed:** exclusive pass times on a backend that doesn't overlap passes; on Metal a serial figure is a split of the frame, not a cost.
2. **Desktop-High's district fight holds with headroom (carried from M5's exit test 1).** `district-fight`'s GPU frame p95 is at or under **11.4 ms, 5% under its 12.0 ms row,** on each of two valid rounds, and every other tier's §3.2 and §3.3 rows still hold as M5's final run judged them. The 5% margin, 0.6 ms, is a proposal (§3): it is larger than the 0.1 to 0.5 ms gains that sit inside the run-to-run spread (§6), so a pass doesn't rest on luck.
   - **Proved here:** the Mac at Desktop-High's settings.
   - **Owed:** Desktop-High's reference device, and whether 5% is margin enough on it.
3. **The fight that ships is measured, and holds (new).** The district fight with combat effects and cues on, a cut-in casting the ultimate, in the district with a probe grid, reflection probes and the sun up (B4, B5), has rows on all four tiers, and each tier's GPU frame p95 is at or under its line: 12, 14, 13 and 26 ms.
   - **Proved here:** the Mac at each tier's settings, on the generated effects pack and cast.
   - **Owed:** a phone's frame; authored effects. Desktop-High's estimate today is 13 to 15 ms, so this is the test most likely to need §5's gate after batch 2.
4. **The CPU tails sit under their rows (new).** On a valid run, read at the worst frame and not at p95, on purpose, since the tails are the point: Desktop-Low's secondary motion under its 0.5 ms row (1.57 ms worst at v0.2.0) and Mobile-Low's under its 1.0 (1.53); Desktop-Low's texture streaming under its 0.5 ms row (0.815 worst); and no fight's restart holding a frame, so the restart's row (B3) reads no frame with more than 1 ms of restart in it (a proposal; 7.85 to 7.88 ms at v0.2.0). Every v0.2.0 figure here is [M, logs]; B3 puts them in the report.
   - **Proved here:** the Mac's cores at each tier's settings, with B7's phone-shaped pool beside the phone tiers.
   - **Owed:** a phone's cores, slower by a factor nobody has measured.
5. **The phones' frames fall (new).** Mobile-High's and Mobile-Low's district fight GPU frames fall at p50 by at least the share F1's and F2's probes price for the steps that land, a figure written into the runbook from B9 before the work lands, never a guess; F1's flat path is byte-exact, so no golden moves by it; and the edge upscaler's line gate passes (`look_tiers::the_edge_upscaler_beats_bilinear_at_each_phone_floor`).
   - **Proved here:** the Mac at the phone tiers' settings.
   - **Owed:** a phone's frame and its power, which the folded tail exists to save.
6. **The look holds (carried).** Every golden is byte-identical except where a package's row says it re-baselines, with the FLIP bound the row states. Outlines and the two-tone edge are unchanged at every level switch (`render::level_switches`, the testkit's gates), and the phones' line gate passes.
   - **Proved here:** Metal, lavapipe and WARP.
   - **Owed:** a person's eye on motion: S1's latched step, P4's snapped level, P5's switches, E2's cut-in aliasing.
7. **No hitches and no pipeline growth (carried).** At the commit the final batch measures: `zero_hitches` cold and warm on Metal; the district fight cold and warm (`the_district_encounter_plays_with_no_hitch_cold_and_warm`); the sandbox's replays tests on Metal; and the ten "How to add" recipes walked with no Rust step. M5 ran these last at its rehearsal, not at the commit it measured. Each tier's pipeline inventory is no larger than at v0.2.0 (B0 records it) plus what a row declares, and the cold warm-up is no slower.
   - **Proved here:** Metal on this Mac, and lavapipe in CI by counts.
   - **Owed:** a phone driver's cold compile.
8. **Patches stop holding the boot screen (new).** With packs capped (K2), the 5.6 MB patch that held the boot screen 5.4 to 5.6 s against the district's 1.12 GB pack (M5's M7 spike) holds it under 0.6 s. The target is an estimate; the spike's script measures it.
   - **Proved here:** the Mac, from a loopback host.
   - **Owed:** a phone's storage, and a patch over a real network.

**Feel stays unknown.** Nobody has played anything, and M6 doesn't change that. Everything it builds serves any game on this engine.

Ranked. Each has a measure a test, a bench or the report can read.

| # | Pillar | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Measured before built.** Every package names the measured row it acts on, a probe that prices it before the work, and a kill criterion that stops it. | 2 | **Judged on the frame.** Gains are judged on `gpu frame` p95 from valid rounds first, since Metal's pass rows overlap and a saving can move into the next row; a pass row is read beside it, never alone. | B1's per-round tables. Exit tests 2 and 3. |
| 3 | **The look holds.** An exact change proves it with byte-identical goldens. A change that can't be exact says so in its row, re-baselines only the scenes it names, and states its FLIP bound. Lines and the two-tone edge are never traded. | Exit test 6. |

| 7 | **Presentation moves no checksum.** | No committed replay moves with any package on or off. |

### The cooker's rules

Every new cooked representation keeps these:

1. **A measured row first:** the row it acts on, a probe that prices it before the cooker work (often a run-time override that fakes the cooked result), and a kill criterion.
2. **A format version and a migration:** the importer's stage version so the content cache cooks it again (as `tools/sapphire_pipeline/src/character_maps/layer_mask.rs:29-31` does), the reader's document or asset version, and an old layout read for one release or refused with a clear error. Never a silent layout change.
3. **The golden policy, in the change:** which scenes move, exactly or by a re-baseline with a FLIP bound. A representation that claims exactness proves it with goldens that don't move.
4. **A cook-time budget per platform:** a `budget` cook is already minutes of the whole machine and about 1.1 GB of packs a cook ([m6-handoff.md](m6-handoff.md), "The benchmark"). A step adds under 10% to the cook, or caches by its input.
5. **The patch effect:** a format bump cooks every asset of its kind into the next patch, so until the pack cap (K2) lands a format change ships with a release, not a patch.
6. **A pipeline budget:** each `MaterialFlags` bit (`crates/sapphire_assets/src/material/mod.rs`) can multiply the family's pipeline keys (`crates/sapphire_assets/src/pipeline_key.rs`). A representation that reuses an existing branch is preferred to one that adds a permutation.
7. **Phone memory:** Mobile-Low peaks at 1,024 of its 1,638 MiB ([m5-exit-run.md](m5-exit-run.md), exit test 2). Anything resident counts its bytes on each phone tier.
8. **Determinism:** cooks are byte-identical across machines today (three machines cooked the same sets, [m5-plan.md](m5-plan.md), D0's row). A new step uses no thread that reorders output, no wall clock and no unseeded random number, and adds a byte-identity test.

### What not to do

Each idea moves a cost this engine doesn't pay, or blurs the lines and band edges that make the look. Each has what would reopen it.

| Idea | Why not | Reopen when |
|---|---|---|
| Variable-rate or half-rate shading, lines at a higher rate than fills | wgpu 30 has no variable-rate API; the two-tone split takes `fwidth`, so a 2×2 rate staircases it; the best published gains fall off at 1080p [U]. | A world-only half-rate prototype that holds line contrast. |
| Temporal reuse of lines | Moving lines keep no history (`temporal.rs:40-51`). | Never for the look. |
| The GPU-driven path: GPU culling and indirect draws, meshlets, mesh shaders, a visibility buffer, virtual geometry, micro-triangle clusters | `render` is about 1 ms at 2,000 to 4,000 calls [M, logs]; wgpu 30's Metal multi-draw is a CPU loop; the `lods` pair shows triangle cuts convert poorly into time on this GPU. D11 was cut for the same reason ([m6-handoff.md](m6-handoff.md), "Cut from M5"). | `render` over a few ms on a phone at shipping counts. |

### What wgpu 30 allows

Read from wgpu 30.0.1's source and changelog [U]: memoryless transient attachments on Metal and Vulkan (a no-op on D3D12), in-pass MSAA resolve, a discarding store and an undefined load, `SHADER_F16` on all three backends (requested by the engine, used by no shader), subgroups, immediates, dual-source blending, render bundles, and timestamp queries inside passes, though not on Apple GPUs, which time only at pass boundaries. Every package in §4 fits inside that: the interface into the last render pass, effects into a second resolve output, the formats and the half-float spike are portable.

## 4. Work packages

Each package is one pull request into `main`, or a few. Sizes are as §3 says. Each finished package gets an "As built" note in its row and in its spec §5 section; a killed one gets "As built: killed", its measurement and why.

The usual rules carry over:

- Existing goldens stay byte-identical unless a row says otherwise, and an old scene pins a new feature off.
- A new scene commits only a Metal golden.
- A feature builds its pipelines only when its work can run, declares them to the inventory, and joins the warm-up and `declares_every_setting`.
- Every system publishes a stat counter (R9) against its budget.
- No checksum moves unless a row says so.
- Each new document struct denies unknown fields.
- A cook step stays on the deterministic maths, so cooks agree across architectures.
- A rule from [m6-handoff.md](m6-handoff.md)'s "Rules the reviews made" binds whatever it touches.
- A look package's row names its four tiers: Desktop-High, Desktop-Low, Mobile-High and Mobile-Low.

M6's own, for every row below:

- **Measure first** names the probe that prices the package before it is built: a B9 run, a unit B4 to B8 adds, or a B6 switch. **Kill if** names the criterion that stops it. **Done when** is what lands it.
- Gains are ranges with their marks. A package's gain is judged on `gpu frame` p95 from valid rounds, alternated against its base in one sitting.
- Agents prove a package with counts, tests and goldens, and hand over the timed script; the orchestrator runs every timed figure, in a quiet window.

## 5. Order

Fifty packages in four batches. Within a batch, packages that don't depend on each other run as parallel pull requests.

1. **Measurement, and what needs none.** B0 first and alone, at v0.2.0's code, since any M6 change moves what it measures. Then B1, B2, B3, X1, K1 and B6, with X4 after B0.
2. **The probes and the cheap measured wins.** B9, with B4, B5, B7 and B8, whose units join its manifest as they land. Then, each on its probe: S1, P1, P2, F1, J1, J2, J3, K2, and X2 beside them.
3. **Each on its probe:** P3, P4, K3 with P5, K4, F2 to F8, E1 to E4, J4 to J7, P6, P7, S2 and X3.
4. **Exit:** Z1, Z2, Z3 and Z4.

**The gate after batch 2.** B9's and B5's figures can reorder everything after them, as M5's baseline did: a package whose probe kills it lands its measurement, and one whose row turns out larger gains its priority. If B5 reads Desktop-High's shipped fight over its 12 ms line by more than batch 3's priced packages can recover, the plan stops and asks the maintainer (§7): another line for exit test 3, an effects budget for the content, or dynamic resolution on by default.

**Critical path:** B0 → B1 → B9 → S1, P1, P2 → P4 → K4 → Z1 → Z2 → Z4. The fight that ships, B4 → B5 → E1 to E4 and F6, starts in batch 2 beside it.

**Shader packages land one at a time.** P1, P2, P3, P4, K4 and P7 edit the anime character shader and the hull's, which include each other, so they land in that order, each rebased and proved on the last; F1 and F2 edit the upscaler's in a line of their own.

## 6. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Metal overlaps its passes,** so a pass row is a tail and a saving can hide under another pass or move into the next row. | Gains are judged on the GPU frame first (§2); B2's serial rows split the frame; exclusive costs stay owed to a Vulkan or DX12 GPU. |
| **Gains of 0.1 to 0.5 ms sit inside the run-to-run spread.** | Arms alternated in one sitting, two valid rounds where a verdict rests on it, and B1's per-round tables, so a figure is never a least taken across rounds. |
| **Rounds go void by the display's lock.** | Timed runs only with the Mac idle and the display asleep; B1's significance reading beside the fixed rule; a void round costs a run again, never a false pass. |
| **The shipped fight may be over its line by more than the packages recover** (13 to 15 ms estimated on Desktop-High). | B5 measures it in batch 2, and §5's gate asks the maintainer before batch 3 is planned around a line it can't meet. |
| **The generated cast flatters.** Its control maps are constant per region and its maps are scaled-up paints, so it under-represents what authored texture detail costs, and K4 relies on it. | Rows that rely on it say so; K1 re-runs on authored content; Z3 reviews the benchmark for what it hides. |
| **No phone has run anything,** and the phones' limit (vertices, pixels or bandwidth) is unknown and decides P5, F2, the colour target and ASTC. | Phones ranked by share; no go or kill on a phone millisecond; X3 makes the session one command. |
| **Re-baselined goldens** hide a regression among the moves. | Each row names its scenes and FLIP bound before the work; an exact change must be byte-identical; `render::level_switches` and the testkit's line gates hold lines and edges throughout. |
| **Pipeline count and warm-up grow** with switches and variants. | B6's switches are override constants at their shipping defaults, held by a test; exit test 7 counts every tier's inventory against B0's baseline. |
| **wgpu:** a patch release can move a golden; f16 and serial timestamps may behave differently on the three backends. | X1 lands first with its goldens checked on three backends; P7 is a spike before it is a package; B2's rows are read as splits. |
| **Shader packages collide** in files that include each other. | They land one at a time in §5's order. |
| **The CPU packages touch determinism.** | Springs and restarts are presentation or keep their state; every replay is run with each change on and off. |
